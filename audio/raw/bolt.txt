Watch the cloth before you read anything.

This roll is a tahn-moh-noh, a bolt. In the old reckoning, one bolt is one kimono.

Its width is settled before any tailor begins. In sixteen thirty-six the shogunate went further, and fixed by law how long and how wide a bolt of silk had to be.

Keep that width in mind. The width of every part that follows derives from it.

Now the cut. There is no paper pattern. The tailor only divides.

Straight cuts give eight pieces. Every strip has a place, and nothing is thrown away.

Then the pieces are joined again, by hand.

The stitches are sewn so that they barely show on the face. Follow the seams: they run straight, because every piece is straight.

There is no dart, and no curve. Hung on its rack, the robe is as flat as the bolt it came from.

And yet it has to close around a person. Come round to the front, and see how.
