Look at the loom first, then at the robes. Across the year, nothing about their cut changes. The sleeves keep their length. The skirt is never shortened.

What changes is the cloth. Three Japanese design researchers put it plainly: the shape is the same from formal wear to everyday wear. The fabric, and what is dyed or woven into it, decides the use.

Changing one wardrobe for the next has its own name, koh-roh-moh-gah-eh. Druet counts it among the care rituals, beside airing a whole collection at once.

Is summer kimono cool? A Japanese laboratory study dressed women in summer kimono in a hot room. The robe is full of openings: collar, cuffs, sleeve slits, hem. Even so, over the stomach the humidity passed ninety per cent. In one wearer's trials, silk stayed drier than polyester.

The season is also worn early. A summer robe may already carry the bellflower of autumn. Further along this wall, the pattern begins to say more than the month.
