Start with the small repeat, then walk on to the large designs. In the ceremonial kind, guides say, the design runs across the seams as one picture, and the motifs gather below the oh-bee and at the left shoulder.

Robes of this age are rare. Koide explains why: the koh-soh-deh was a thing to be worn out. So she counted robes in paintings instead, nearly a thousand of them.

Her finding is that no style stood alone. Each borrowed elements from the others, and in exchange made the next.

Koide ties the kahn-boon style to a wider public. As the shogun's order settled, townspeople came to enjoy the koh-soh-deh, in quality and in number.

Today the code is read less closely. Asked about the flowers on her own foo-ree-soh-deh, one young woman told Becke: flowers are flowers, right? She had chosen by colour. In the next room, the flat picture meets a body.
