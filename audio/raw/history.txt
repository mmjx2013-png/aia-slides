Begin before any print on this wall. At the Hay-ahn court, by the mid-twelfth century, the koh-soh-deh was underwear, worn next to the skin. As court dress grew simpler, the robes above it came off, and the koh-soh-deh came to the surface.

By the early eh-doh period its shape was not far from today's kimono. A pattern book printed in sixteen sixty-six shows the large, flowing designs of the kahn-boon style: a wide empty back with large, moving motifs.
