# JBL (Jabil Inc) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: inputs file only (10-K accession 0001628280-26-067038, FY ended 2026-08-31, filed 2026-10-23; XBRL screen; price pack; labelled headlines). Excerpts cover Item 7 (first 24,000 characters) and Item 1A (first 14,000 characters) only. Anything not in those is "not in the filing excerpt".

## Verdict
WATCH. A high-quality EMS operator on a fair multiple for a cyclical, with thin margins, FCF flattered by payables timing, and half of revenue now riding on AI data-center programs from a few customers. The price has fallen 37% in 6 months but the multiple is still above its own history, so it is not yet cheap enough.

## Business
Jabil is a contract manufacturer and supply-chain/engineering services provider. It designs, builds, assembles and delivers products for other companies, and buys the components itself (turnkey), so revenue is mostly pass-through materials with a thin margin on top (10-K 0001628280-26-067038, Item 7, Overview). Three segments, FY2026 share of net revenue (Item 7, Net Revenue):
- Intelligent Infrastructure, 50% (41% in FY25, 32% in FY24): AI and cloud/data-center infrastructure, networking and communications, capital equipment. Revenue +45% in FY26, with cloud/data center up 33% from existing customers, "primarily due to strong demand for certain AI infrastructure programs".
- Regulated Industries, 35% (40%, 42%): automotive and transportation, healthcare and packaging, renewable and energy infrastructure. Revenue +7%.
- Connected Living and Digital Commerce, 15% (19%, 26%): warehouse automation, robotics, consumer-linked. Revenue -4%; connected living -11%. Renamed Intelligent Devices and Robotics from 2026-09-01.
Foreign-source revenue was 73.1% of the total (75.0% FY25, 82.5% FY24). The mix has shifted heavily toward one end market in two years.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Scale in global procurement and manufacturing, and integrated design-to-delivery services across the product life cycle (Item 7, Overview).
- ROIC 35.9% and ROE 64.6% (XBRL screen); the ROIC is high because the asset base is lean and working capital is partly funded by suppliers.
- Regulated markets (healthcare, auto, energy) have qualification hurdles that make switching slow. This is my inference; the excerpt does not quantify it.

Evidence against:
- Customers "generally do not commit to long-term production schedules" and most do not commit to firm schedules beyond one quarter; they have cancelled and resourced before (Item 1A, first risk factor).
- Gross margin 9.2%, operating margin about 4.7%, R&D $30M or 0.1% of revenue (Item 7). There is little pricing power or proprietary technology; margins come from mix and utilization.
- Competition from "numerous other diversified manufacturing service providers" (Item 1A).
Net: a durable operating franchise with good returns, but a narrow moat. Customers hold the bargaining power.

## Numbers
FY ended 2026-08-31 unless noted.
- Revenue $35,954M, +20.6% vs FY25 $29,802M; FY24 $28,883M (10-K, Item 7, Summary of Results). Three-year revenue CAGR 1.2% (XBRL screen), so FY26 is a jump after a flat stretch.
- Gross profit $3,317M, 9.2% (8.9% FY25, 9.3% FY24) (10-K, Item 7, Gross Profit).
- Operating income $1,704M, 4.74% margin, calculated from the figures above (10-K, Item 7). FY25 was $1,182M (3.97%); FY24 was $2,013M (6.97%). FY26 operating income is still below FY24 despite 24% more revenue.
- Net income to Jabil $1,042M; diluted EPS $9.75 (FY25 $5.92, FY24 $11.17) (10-K, Item 7).
- SG&A $1,222M (up $207M, partly from the Hanley and Rebound Technologies acquisitions); restructuring $97M (FY25 $181M, FY24 $296M); stock-based compensation $140M (FY25 $107M); amortization of intangibles $89M (10-K, Item 7).
- Free cash flow $1,374M, margin 3.82%; five-year average FCF margin 3.25% with standard deviation 0.76 points (XBRL screen). FCF CAGR 25.0% (XBRL screen).
- Net debt: about $2.14B, derived from net debt / FCF of 1.56x on FCF of $1,374M; net debt / EBITDA 0.90x (XBRL screen). Gross debt and cash balances are not in the filing excerpt. Leverage is comfortable.
- Share count: dilution metric -21.3% (XBRL screen), meaning the share count has shrunk, from buybacks; the measurement window is not in the filing excerpt. Implied current shares about 103M (market cap $23.49B / $228.14).
- Market cap $23.49B; EV about $25.6B, derived (XBRL screen).
- Working capital, Q4 FY26 vs Q4 FY25 (10-K, Item 7, Key Performance Indicators): sales cycle 2 days vs 18; days in payables 135 vs 96; days in receivables 55 vs 44; days in inventory 82 vs 69; inventory turns 4 vs 5.

Quality-of-cash note: the cash conversion cycle collapsed from 18 days to 2 days, and the excerpt attributes the payables increase to "timing of payments in the Intelligent Infrastructure segment". An extra 39 days of payables on roughly $8-9B of quarterly cost of revenue (my estimate, not in the filing) is a very large cash tailwind. If it reverses, reported FCF falls sharply. The reported $1.37B probably overstates the clean run-rate. This is the most important accounting point in the memo.

## Why is it cheap?
Stock last $228.14 (2026-11-25): 1 month -19%, 6 months -37%, 1 year +16%, 3 years +73%; drawdown from the 5-year high -41%; volatility 50% a year (price pack). It is "cheap" only against its recent peak. The worries:
1. AI capex digestion. Half of revenue, and nearly all the growth, is AI/cloud infrastructure with a handful of customers. The market is pricing a risk that these programs slow or are resourced. The worry is reasonable: customers can cancel on short notice, and AI infrastructure orders are lumpy.
2. Margin ceiling. Operating margin of 4.7% is thin, and rising component prices (including a global memory shortage, Item 1A) can hit margins between repricings.
3. Cash-flow quality, as above.
4. Sentiment reset after a big run (the "293% five year run" headline). Baird cut its target from $440 to $270 in the last week.
My view: the worry about AI concentration is right to the extent that the multiple should not be a peak-cycle multiple. The stock is not obviously mispriced. EV/EBIT is 15.0x, 39% above its own history by the screen's ratio (1.391), so the market is still paying for the AI mix. A drawdown from a hot level is not the same as a bargain.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = True, so the normalized case is the base case.
- Normalized FCF yield 4.98% (XBRL screen), against 5.85% on reported FY26 FCF. That implies normalized FCF of about $1.17B (the 3.25% average margin applied to $35.95B revenue).
- Normalized EV/EBIT 14.18x vs 15.04x reported (XBRL screen).
- My stricter view: if working-capital timing reverses and the mix reverts, FCF could sit nearer the lower end of the historical margin band (about 2.5% on the screen's own mean minus one standard deviation, roughly $0.9B on FY26 revenue). That gives a yield near 3.8% on the current market cap. This is an illustration, not a forecast.
- Operating income has already shown the cycle: $2,013M (FY24), $1,182M (FY25), $1,704M (FY26). Peak to trough was -41%.

## Disruption risk
Not a software, payments or internet name. The relevant technology risk is customers shifting AI hardware sourcing (including to other manufacturers or in-house) and the need for Jabil to keep pace with machine-learning-enabled manufacturing (Item 1A).

## Risks
Top 5 from Item 1A, most important first:
1. Customer ordering and cancellation risk. Customers do not commit beyond about one quarter, can cancel or resource, and fixed costs mean a demand drop quickly hurts margins and leaves excess and obsolete inventory.
2. Customer concentration. A small number of customers supply a significant share of revenue, and the mix is more concentrated in AI infrastructure each year. Losing or shrinking one large program would be material. Customer names and percentages are not in the filing excerpt.
3. Component supply and price. Single-source parts, a current global memory shortage, and the risk of bearing price increases between quarterly repricings; early buying raises inventory and obsolescence exposure.
4. Growth and ramp-up strain. Rapid growth, capex ahead of customer payment, and delayed ramps hurt cash flow. FY26 showed this pattern in the working-capital swings.
5. Emerging-company customers and technology change. Extended payment terms and loans to newer customers raise credit risk; failing to keep pace with technology and new business models would reduce demand. Cloud customers also have different demand cycles that complicate working capital.
Other (not ranked): tariffs. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20 and refunds began, with no material impact on results (Item 7). The tariff landscape "continues to shift".

## Valuation
- FCF yield 5.85% reported, 4.98% normalized (XBRL screen). A low-teens price/FCF multiple is not demanding for a business with 36% ROIC, but only if the FCF is clean.
- P/E about 23x on diluted EPS of $9.75 at $228.14 (my calculation). FY24 EPS was $11.17 on lower revenue, so earnings are not at a record level and the multiple is not low.
- EV/EBIT 15.0x reported, 14.2x normalized; the screen's ratio to its own history is 1.39x, so it trades about 39% above its historical level. Valuation sub-score is 0.44, below the 0.5 midpoint (XBRL screen).
- Priced in: durable AI-driven growth and a margin structure above the historical 4-7% band. The stock needs operating income to keep growing to justify 15x. Analyst average target of $418 (MarketBeat headline) is stale next to the $228 price and Baird's $270, so I give it no weight.
- Composite screen score 0.70 (quality 0.75, momentum 0.81, balance 0.65, valuation 0.44, growth 0.53, dilution 0.97): strong on quality and buybacks, weak on valuation. Momentum is high on the 12-1 measure (+43.8%) but the last six months are sharply negative.

## Thesis
What would make this a BUY-candidate:
- AI infrastructure programs convert into repeatable, higher-margin revenue; gross margin held at 9.2% or better despite the mix shift (FY26 mix improved gross margin).
- Share count is falling (-21% on the screen) while leverage stays low (0.9x EBITDA).
- ROIC of 36% suggests an efficient capital allocator; buybacks at lower prices are accretive.
- Regulated Industries (35% of revenue, +7%) gives a partial cycle buffer.
- A price near 11-12x normalized EBIT or a normalized FCF yield above 6% would give a margin of safety; neither is the case today.

## What breaks the thesis
Checkable signals:
1. Gross margin below 8.5% for two consecutive quarters (FY26 was 9.2%).
2. Intelligent Infrastructure revenue declining year on year in any quarter, or its share of revenue falling while total revenue stalls.
3. Days in payables falling back toward 100 (from 135) with a sales cycle above 15 days (from 2), and operating cash flow dropping as a result; FY26 FCF below $1.0B.
4. Operating margin back below 4.0% (FY25 was 3.97%).
5. Inventory days above 90 with inventory turns of 4 or below, together with new inventory write-downs; or a disclosed loss or cut of a top customer program.

## If AI data-centre capex falls 30% next year
- Exposure: Intelligent Infrastructure is 50% of revenue, 0.50 x $35,954M = about $17,977M (10-K, Item 7). Cloud/data-centre alone is not sized in the excerpt, but it drove 33 of the segment's 45 points of growth, and the filing ties growth to "certain AI infrastructure programs". Is more than 30% of revenue tied to data-centre/cloud/AI? Probably yes, but not provable from the excerpt; 50% is the upper bound. Customer names and percentages: not in the filing excerpt.
- Revenue (upper bound, whole segment falls 30%): -0.30 x $17,977M = -$5,393M, or -15.0% of total, leaving about $30.6B.
- Margin: at the 9.2% gross margin the loss is $496M; assuming 15% decremental margin (fixed costs, my assumption) it is $809M. Operating income $1,704M falls to about $1,208M-$895M; margin 3.9%-2.9% (from 4.7%).
- FCF: lower profit trims about $0.4-0.65B after tax, leaving roughly $0.7-1.0B before working capital. A payables unwind (135 days) not offset by lower receivables and inventory could push it negative for a year; on earnings alone it stays positive.
