# PSX (Phillips 66): paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH: a decent integrated downstream business at a mid-single-digit normalized FCF yield, but the stock is up 82% in a year and 74% above the Q4 2025 buyback price, so the "cheap" case is gone and the thesis rests on refining margins we cannot forecast.

## Business
Phillips 66 describes itself as "a leading integrated downstream energy provider" with six segments: Midstream, Chemicals, Refining, Marketing and Specialties (M&S), and Renewable Fuels (10-K 0001534701-26-000006, Item 7 Executive Overview). Total assets were $73.7 billion at year-end 2025 (same section).
- Refining: turns purchased crude into fuels. It buys all its feedstock and produces no crude (Item 1A, Competition Risks). It targets a clean product yield above 86% (Item 7, Strategic Priorities). It is converting the San Francisco refinery into the Rodeo Complex, a renewable-fuels site (Item 1A, Strategic Performance).
- Midstream: gathering, processing, pipelines, fractionation and terminals for natural gas, NGL and crude. It holds a stake in Dakota Access (Item 3).
- Chemicals: run entirely through a joint venture (CPChem, per the CEO biography in the executive officers table; Item 1A, Joint Ventures).
- M&S: wholesale marketing and retail of refined products.
- Renewable Fuels: processes used cooking oil, vegetable oils and waste oils into low-carbon fuels (Item 1A).
Customers are industrial, commercial and retail fuel buyers, plus upstream producers who use the midstream services. Segment revenue and profit splits are not in the filing excerpt.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Scale and integration across refining, midstream and chemicals. Midstream and chemicals fee-like income dampens the refining cycle somewhat. The size of that cushion is not in the filing excerpt.
- Refineries and pipelines are hard to replace. Permitting is slow and the political climate is hostile to new builds (Item 1A, Strategic Performance).
Evidence against:
- Refining is a commodity business with no pricing power. The filing says that for Chemicals "market position cannot necessarily be protected by product differentiation or by passing on cost increases" (Item 1A).
- Competitors with their own crude production or large retail networks "may be better positioned to withstand periods of depressed refining margins" and some have greater financial resources (Item 1A, Competition Risks).
- A large part of earnings sits in joint ventures where PSX shares control (Item 1A).
- ROIC is "nan" in the screen, and ROE is 15.1% for FY2025 (XBRL screen), which does not show a durable excess return through the cycle.
Net: a cost-position and asset-quality business, not a moat business. Returns depend on the margin cycle.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap anymore, and I think that is the main finding. The stock gained 82% in one year and 120% in three. It fell 12% in the last month, which Trefis headlines as eight straight red days, down 10% (headlines). It is still 20% below its 5-year high (price stats).
What the market worries about, from the 10-K:
- Refining margins are cyclical and have already been strong. The price rise suggests the market is paying for a margin peak. If margins fade, FCF falls.
- California exposure: the Los Angeles refinery faced a Clean Water Act case (settled by a deferred prosecution agreement with an $8 million penalty), and the San Francisco refinery was converted to renewable fuels (10-K, Items 3 and 1A).
- The Propel Fuels trade-secret judgment of $833 million, accruing 10% post-judgment interest, is under appeal. The company says it may face losses beyond the amount recorded (10-K, Item 3).
- Falling revenue and FCF over several years (XBRL screen).
Are these worries right? The margin worry is fair and cannot be dismissed. The Propel judgment is about 0.9% of market cap, so it is real but not thesis-breaking. The legal and California issues look manageable. The underlying business is probably fine, but at this price it is not mispriced in our favour.

## Cyclical check
The screen flags cyclical_normalized = True. Normalized FCF is the average FCF margin of 4.45% applied to FY2025 revenue of $132.4 billion, which gives about $5.9 billion. This matches the normalized FCF yield of 6.4% on a $92.1 billion market cap (XBRL screen). FY2025 FCF of $4.96 billion is therefore below the average margin, not a peak year, by this measure. The caveat is that the average margin covers only the history in the screen, which I cannot see, and may include a boom period. A through-cycle trough margin could be well below 4.45%. No normalized EBIT is available (EV/EBIT is "nan").

## Disruption risk
Not a software, payments or internet name. The long-term disruption risk is demand loss for petroleum fuels (technological advances and substitutes are listed in Item 1A), which is slow and not a one-year threat.

## Risks
1. Margin cyclicality and volatility. Earnings and cash flow follow the spread between product and feedstock prices, driven by demand, OPEC, geopolitics, tariffs and imports. Low margins could force impairments and curb buybacks and dividends (Item 1A).
2. Operational and hazard risk. Turnarounds, hurricanes, fires, explosions and cyber events at PSX or its affiliates can cut output and bring fines and lawsuits (Item 1A).
3. Legal exposure: the $833 million Propel judgment with 10% interest, plus consent decrees with the EPA that carry stipulated penalties (Item 3).
4. Policy risk for Renewable Fuels: changes to renewable fuel standards, low-carbon fuel standards, tax credits or feedstock eligibility could hurt returns on the Rodeo conversion (Item 1A).
5. Capital projects and joint ventures: large projects can miss budget or returns, and JV partners may have conflicting goals, with a whole segment (Chemicals) run through a JV (Item 1A). Midstream also needs new gas and NGL supply to offset natural decline.

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 5.39% on FY2025 FCF; 6.40% normalized (XBRL screen). Valuation sub-score is 0.65 and momentum is 0.95 (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT vs own history: not available ("nan" in the screen). I cannot say whether it trades above or below its own history.
- What is priced in: at $236 the stock needs refining margins at or near recent levels and continued buybacks. A 6.4% normalized yield is fair for a cyclical but not a bargain. A fall in the margin cycle back to a 3% FCF margin would take the yield on current market cap to roughly 4.3% (3% x $132.4 billion = $4.0 billion; $4.0 billion / $92.1 billion), which would be expensive.
- The 12-1 momentum of +105.8% (XBRL screen) shows the re-rating has already happened.

## Thesis
- Integrated downstream and midstream assets at scale, with fee-like midstream income softening the refining cycle.
- Normalized FCF yield of about 6.4% with a shrinking share count, if the screen's dilution figure is read correctly.
- Dividend and buyback capacity: 2025 payouts were $3.1 billion against $4.96 billion FCF (10-K, Item 7).
- The recent 12% pullback leaves the stock 20% below its 5-year high, so a better entry could come.
- The Propel and Clean Water Act matters are bounded relative to a $92 billion market cap.
Why only WATCH: the thesis depends on a margin forecast we cannot make from this file, FCF has been declining (CAGR -22.9%), EV/EBIT and net debt are unverified, and the stock has already doubled in three years.

## What breaks the thesis
- Normalized FCF margin below about 3% on the next two annual filings (the 4.45% average is what the 6.4% yield assumes).
- Quarterly operating cash flow in the 10-Q/10-K running below capital spending plus dividends (about $2.2 billion capex and $1.9 billion dividends a year in 2025) for two quarters in a row.
- A reduction in buyback pace below the 2025 level of $1.2 billion a year, or a dividend cut.
- An adverse ruling on the Propel appeal that raises the total judgment well above $833 million, or a new damages award in the attorney's fees motion that is material.
- Net debt rising materially from the 2025 level, once we confirm that level (the screen's figure looks unreliable).
- Policy changes cutting Renewable Fuels credits or feedstock eligibility, leading to an impairment on the Rodeo Complex.
