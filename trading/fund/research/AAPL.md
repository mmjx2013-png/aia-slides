# AAPL (Apple Inc.) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH. Exceptional business and cash machine, but at 33x EV/EBIT and a 2.6% FCF yield, with FY2026 flattered by one-offs, the price already pays for the quality; no margin of safety.

## Business
Apple designs and sells consumer hardware and a services layer on top of it. FY2026 net sales were $478.8B (10-K 0000320193-26-000026, Item 7, Segment/Products tables):
- iPhone $254.1B (53%, +21%), driven by Pro models.
- Services $123.3B (26%, +13%): advertising, cloud and other; the 10-K says growth came mainly from advertising and cloud.
- Mac $37.9B (+13%), iPad $27.9B (flat), Wearables, Home and Accessories $35.6B (flat).
- Geography: Americas $198.1B, Europe $127.6B, Greater China $82.4B (+28% after -4% in FY2025), Japan $30.9B, Rest of Asia Pacific $39.9B. Sales outside the US are a majority (Item 1A).
Customers are mostly consumers, sold through own stores, carriers, resellers and online. Product launches in FY2026 included iPhone 18 Pro, a foldable-style "iPhone Duo", MacBook Neo, and "Siri AI" (Item 7).

## Moat
For:
- Integrated hardware, OS, software and services: the 10-K says Apple designs "nearly the entire solution" (Item 1A).
- Pricing power and margin: total gross margin 48.8% vs 46.9% and 46.2% in the prior two years; Services gross margin 76.2% (Item 7).
- ROIC 76% and ROE 118% (XBRL screen); FCF margin stable at 26.5% average with a std of 1.8 points (XBRL screen).
- Installed base feeds Services; Services is 26% of sales but 40% of gross margin ($93.9B of $233.6B, derived from Item 7).
Against / caveats:
- Apple says it has a minority market share in smartphones, PCs, tablets and wearables (Item 1A), so the moat is brand and ecosystem, not scale dominance.
- Wearables and iPad were flat in FY2026; growth was concentrated in iPhone.
- Regulatory and litigation risk can force changes to services and IP use (Item 1A), which targets the Services profit pool.
- Heavy reliance on third-party manufacturing and single-source components.
Net: strong, durable moat; the debate is price, not quality.

## Numbers (FY ended 2026-09-26)
- Revenue $478.8B, +15% vs $416.2B (10-K, Item 7; XBRL screen shows 478,834M). Three-year revenue CAGR 7.7% (XBRL screen).
- Gross margin $233.6B, 48.8% (10-K, Item 7).
- Operating expenses $75.5B (R&D $45.8B, +32%; SG&A $29.8B, +8%) (10-K, Item 7).
- Operating income: not stated in the excerpt; derived as $233.6B - $75.5B = about $158.1B, 33.0% margin. Cross-check: tax $27.1B at 17.0% implies pretax about $159B (10-K, Item 7), consistent.
- FCF $136.1B, margin 28.4% (XBRL screen); FCF CAGR 11% (XBRL screen).
- Net debt: the screen shows net debt/FCF 0.38 and net debt/EBITDA 0.30 (XBRL screen), implying roughly $52B of net debt. The 10-K excerpt shows cash and marketable securities of $136.9B against notes principal of $78.7B plus $2.0B commercial paper (Item 7), which would be net cash of about $56B. I cannot reconcile the two from the excerpt (the screen may include leases, other liabilities or exclude long-term securities); either way leverage is trivial against $136B of FCF. Flag for the PM.
- Capital return: $85.8B buybacks and $15.7B dividends in FY2026 (about $101.5B, 75% of FCF); new $100B buyback authorized April 2026; quarterly dividend $0.27 (10-K, Item 7).
- Share count: the screen's dilution figure is -6.9% (XBRL screen; period and definition not in the excerpt), meaning shares are shrinking. Exact share count: not in the filing excerpt.
- Market cap $5.167T (XBRL screen). Tax rate 17.0% vs 15.6% (10-K, Item 7).

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap. Valuation score is the weakest of the screen components (s_valuation 0.36 vs quality 0.86; XBRL screen). The stock is at its 5-year high (drawdown 0%), +29% in 1 year and +88% in 3 years (price stats). What the market seems to be paying for: the iPhone upgrade cycle (iPhone +21%), Services compounding, a Greater China rebound, and a perceived defensive status when AI names sold off (headlines). The worries that exist are second order: memory and storage cost inflation, tariffs, regulation and AI spending. My view: the quality is real, but the multiple has expanded on a year of favourable one-offs, so the worry that matters is "what if growth reverts to mid single digits at 33x EBIT".

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = False, so no normalization applied. But I do not take FY2026 as a clean base:
- Products gross margin rose to 39.3% from 36.8% partly on "tariff refunds" recognized in cost of sales (Item 7). A refund is non-recurring and the 10-K warns refunds may not be collectable if tariffs are rescinded (Item 1A). The size of the refund is not in the filing excerpt.
- iPhone +21% follows +4%; a new-product-cycle spike. Greater China +28% follows -4%.
- Management itself says gross margins "will be subject to volatility and downward pressure" and expects memory and storage supply constraints to intensify (Item 7).
Sensitivity (my estimate, not from the filing): if FY2027 revenue growth reverts to the 3-year CAGR of about 7.7% and gross margin gives back about 1.5 points, operating income would be roughly $160-165B, essentially flat on FY2026, as R&D (+32%) also keeps rising.

## Disruption risk
AI is the main one: Apple is spending more (R&D +32% on AI infrastructure) and has launched "Siri AI" but trails frontier model vendors; if agents displace app and device interaction, the App Store / Services take rate and device pull are exposed. Payments and App Store regulation are a second channel (Item 1A mentions regulatory changes to products and services).

## Risks (top 5 from Item 1A, most important first)
1. Trade and tariffs plus supply concentration: most manufacturing is done by partners in China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam, with single or limited sources for chips, memory and storage; new tariffs, Section 232 / 301 actions and export controls can raise cost or block products, and refunds may not be collected.
2. Component cost and gross margin pressure: memory, storage and advanced chip supply-demand imbalance; price increases may not offset and may cut demand.
3. Intense competition and need for constant innovation: aggressive rivals, short product cycles, large R&D that may not pay back (AI spend).
4. Regulation, litigation and IP: governments can force product or service changes, require sharing of innovations, or limit IP value; this directly targets high-margin Services.
5. Macro and geopolitical shocks, natural disasters and cyber events: consumer demand is discretionary, a majority of sales are international, and operations sit in earthquake-prone areas. (The remaining Item 1A text, including legal and Services-specific risks, was cut off in the excerpt.)

## Valuation
- FCF yield 2.63%; normalized 2.46% (XBRL screen). That is below a risk-free-plus-premium bar for a long-only fund, even with 11% FCF CAGR.
- EV/EBIT 33.0; normalized 34.5; 1.13x its own history, i.e. about 13% above its own average (XBRL screen).
- What is priced in: at a 2.5-2.6% yield, a buyer needs FCF to compound at roughly 8-10% a year for a decade with the multiple holding to earn a high-single-digit return (my arithmetic). The record shows 7.7% revenue and 11% FCF growth, helped by buybacks (-6.9% shares). So the price assumes the best recent years persist and the multiple does not compress.
- Momentum is good (12-1 month +26%) but volatility is 25% and the worst 5-year drawdown was -33%; a de-rating from here could cost a third.

## Thesis (what the bull case would be)
- Services (26% of sales, 76% gross margin) keeps growing at low teens, lifting mix.
- iPhone Pro cycle and Greater China recovery sustain mid-to-high single digit growth beyond FY2026.
- FCF of $136B with about 75% returned yearly shrinks the share count by several percent annually.
- Balance sheet is a non-issue; downside from leverage is nil.
- Entry preferred on a pullback; I would want an FCF yield nearer 3.5% (about $3.9T market cap at current FCF, roughly 25% lower) before calling BUY.

## What breaks the thesis (checkable)
- Total gross margin below 46% for two consecutive quarters (FY2026 48.8%; FY2025 46.9%).
- Services growth below 8% year on year for two quarters (FY2026 +13%).
- iPhone revenue down year on year in the two quarters after the iPhone 18 launch (FY2026 +21%).
- Greater China revenue falling back to a decline (FY2026 +28%).
- Loss of tariff refund / new Section 232 semiconductor tariffs that raise cost of sales, or a regulatory ruling that cuts App Store or search-related revenue.
- R&D growth above revenue growth again in FY2027 without visible AI product revenue (FY2026 R&D +32% vs sales +15%).
