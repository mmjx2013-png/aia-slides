# FLEX (Flex Ltd.): paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
**WATCH.** A quality-improving EMS business, but at 3.4% normalized FCF yield and 19-23x EBIT (about 1.4x its own history) after a 59% drawdown, the price still assumes a successful AI-infrastructure re-rating and spin-off. The margin of safety is thin and the stock is extremely volatile (78%/y).

## Business
Flex is a contract electronics manufacturer (EMS) and design/engineering services provider. The inputs file lists it as "Printed Circuit Boards, SIC 36". Item 1A text refers to design and manufacturing services and components for electronics, including power and infrastructure solutions used in data centers. It also mentions medical devices and other end markets.

- Customers: OEMs across many industries, plus hyperscale cloud providers, colocation companies and large enterprise data-center operators for the Cloud and Power Infrastructure businesses (10-K 0000866374-26-000012, Item 1A).
- Ten largest customers were 45%, 44% and 37% of net sales in FY2026, FY2025 and FY2024. No single customer was above 10% (10-K 0000866374-26-000012, Item 1A).
- Strategy event: a planned spin-off of the Cloud and Power Infrastructure businesses (10-K, Item 1A summary). The Nextracker separation is also listed as a risk.
- Segment revenue split and the segment names are not in the filing excerpt. The Item 7 excerpt in the inputs file is truncated to its heading.

## Moat
The evidence is mixed and leans weak to moderate.

For:
- ROIC of 16.6% and ROE of 17.1% are good for an EMS business (XBRL screen).
- The screen shows rising FCF with flat revenue. FCF CAGR is +49.5% against revenue CAGR of -0.7% (XBRL screen), which points to better mix or better working-capital discipline.
- Switching costs exist: qualification and ramp-up requirements make it hard for customers to move programs (10-K, Item 1A).
- Scale and global footprint help.

Against:
- The filing says it "generally do[es] not obtain firm, long-term purchase commitments". Some contracts allow termination for convenience on short notice, and there may be no wind-down payments (10-K, Item 1A).
- It describes its industry as "highly competitive", with customers able to in-source or change sourcing.
- Margins are thin (free cash flow margin of 3.8%). That is typical of contract manufacturing, where pricing power is limited.
- Customer concentration is higher in the Cloud and Power business, which is where the market is paying up.

My view: a good operator with a modest moat. The valuation is carried by the AI-infrastructure growth narrative, not by structural advantage.

## Why is it cheap?
Strictly, it is not cheap. The valuation score is low (0.246) and the stock sits at about 1.4x its own EV/EBIT history. It looks "cheap" only against its own recent price: down 19% in 1 month, down 54% in 6 months and 59% below its 5-year high, yet still up 18% over 1 year and 236% over 3 years.

What the market seems worried about:
- AI-infrastructure enthusiasm fading. A 2026-11-24 headline lumps Flex in with other AI-linked names that "plummet" (TradingView, negative). Flex is a lower-margin manufacturer valued like an AI beneficiary.
- Execution on the planned spin-off of Cloud and Power: costs, timeline and "if at all" (10-K, Item 1A).
- Thin margins and a short-order-book business that can be hit by demand swings, tariffs and trade policy.

Is the worry right? Partly. The business is not collapsing: FCF is growing and leverage is low. But the fall is from a very elevated multiple. After a 59% drawdown the multiple is still above history, so I think the worry about valuation is more right than wrong. The multiple has compressed, but not to a level that clearly prices in a downturn.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = True, so I use the normalized case, not the peak year.
- Normalized FCF margin is 3.0% (5-year-style average from the screen), against 3.77% in the latest year. That gives about $0.84B of FCF, a normalized FCF yield of 3.38% (XBRL screen), against 4.24% on reported FCF.
- Normalized EV/EBIT is 23.2x, against 19.2x on the latest year (XBRL screen). The ratio to history is 1.42x, meaning the stock trades at about a 42% premium to its own past EV/EBIT.
- Conclusion: on mid-cycle earnings the stock is expensive, not cheap. A 3.4% yield on a business with 1.2-point FCF-margin volatility and thin margins is not compensation for the risk.

## Disruption risk
Not a software, payments or internet name. The relevant disruption is customers in-sourcing, or hyperscalers moving power and cooling work to specialists (10-K, Item 1A), plus a possible slowdown in AI data-center capex. Item 1A also lists AI-use risk, but only as a governance/legal item.

## Risks
Top 5 from Item 1A, most important first:
1. **Demand volatility and short commitments.** No firm long-term purchase commitments, short lead times, termination for convenience, and a largely fixed cost base. A downturn hits margins hard through lost overhead absorption and excess inventory.
2. **Customer concentration, especially in Cloud and Power.** The top ten customers are 45% of sales and rising (37% to 44% to 45%). A few hyperscalers and data-center operators drive the growth story.
3. **Spin-off execution.** The Cloud and Power spin-off may not complete on the planned terms or timeline, and carries significant costs. Margin investment in these businesses could also weigh on results.
4. **Tariffs, trade policy and export controls.** These have hurt results in the past and remain open.
5. **Capital intensity and impairment.** Customer-specific equipment could become obsolete, goodwill could be impaired, and working capital needs careful management. Debt is manageable today (about 1.0x EBITDA).

Others listed in the summary, not ranked: cybersecurity, AI-use risk, activist shareholders, share-price volatility, medical-device quality rules, labor, FX, tax.

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 4.24% reported, 3.38% normalized (XBRL screen). I use the normalized figure.
- EV/EBIT: 19.2x on the latest year, 23.2x normalized, about 1.42x the company's own history (XBRL screen).
- What is priced in: continued double-digit growth in data-center power and cloud products, margin expansion beyond the current ~5% operating margin (derived), and a clean spin-off that unlocks value. If revenue stays flat (CAGR -0.7%) and margins stay thin, the yield is below what a long-term holder should accept for a stock with 78% volatility.
- Rough scenario, my estimate: if the multiple normalized to its history (about 1/1.42 of the current level), the stock would be about 30% lower, all else equal. If growth and spin-off value are delivered, FCF could expand enough to justify today's price. The skew is not clearly favorable.
- Position sizing note: even if the thesis were accepted, the volatility argues for a starter weight well below the 8% cap.

## Thesis
For a future BUY, the thesis would be:
- Quality improving: ROIC 16.6%, FCF CAGR +49%, with leverage at about 1.0x EBITDA.
- Capital return: a sharp fall in share count (-18%), which supports per-share FCF.
- AI-infrastructure exposure through Cloud and Power, with a potential value unlock from the spin-off. A UBS buy initiation was reported in the last 30 days.
- Price reset: the stock is -59% from the high, so a better entry may come.

Why I stay at WATCH: the valuation leg is weak and the thesis needs a lower price or evidence of higher mid-cycle earnings.

## What breaks the thesis
Concrete, checkable signals:
- Top-ten customer share rises above about 50% of sales, or a major hyperscaler customer is lost.
- Revenue declines year-on-year for 2 consecutive quarters (the 10-Q for 2026-09-25 should be checked first; its numbers are not in the file).
- Operating margin falls below about 4% (my derived current level is about 4.9%), or FCF margin falls below 3% (the 5-year-style average).
- The spin-off is delayed beyond the stated timeline, cancelled, or its one-time costs are much larger than guided.
- Net debt/EBITDA rises above about 2x, or the share count stops shrinking.

For an upgrade to BUY-candidate, I would want normalized FCF yield above about 5% (price roughly 30% lower, all else equal) or evidence that the normalized FCF margin has moved above 4%.

## If AI data-centre capex falls 30% next year
- Exposed share of revenue: not in the filing excerpt. The segment split is absent (Item 7 is truncated) and Item 1A gives only the top-ten customer share (45% of sales, none above 10%). So I cannot say whether more than 30% of revenue is data-centre/cloud/AI. Unknown.
- Arithmetic per 10% of revenue exposed: 30% x 10% x $27.9B = about $0.84B revenue loss (3% of sales). At my assumed 15-20% decremental margin, EBIT falls $125-170M, about 9-12% of derived EBIT (~$1.37B); operating margin slips from ~4.9% to ~4.6%.
- If 30% were exposed: revenue -$2.5B (-9%), EBIT -$375-500M, margin ~4.1-4.4%.
- FCF ($1.05B) stays positive in both cases (roughly $0.55-0.9B), before any working-capital release or restructuring. Negative FCF would need a far larger fall plus inventory build.
