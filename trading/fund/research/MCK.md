# MCK (McKesson Corporation): paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: the inputs file only (10-K accession 0000927653-26-000069, XBRL screen, price stats, labelled headlines). Anything not in that file is marked "not in the filing excerpt". Research only; no orders.

## Verdict
WATCH: a high-quality, cash-generative distributor, but at about 20.6x EV/EBIT (10% above its own history), a 4.6% FCF yield and a price near its 5-year high after +43% in 6 months, the market already pays for the good news; revisit on a pullback.

## Business
McKesson is a healthcare distribution and services company with fiscal year ending 31 March. FY2026 revenue was $403.4 billion (10-K 0000927653-26-000069, Item 7). It reports four segments after a re-segmentation in Q2 FY2026 (10-K, Item 7 Overview):

- **North American Pharmaceutical**: distribution and logistics of branded, generic, specialty, biosimilar and OTC drugs to US and Canadian pharmacies (retail, hospital, alternate sites), plus pharmacy financial, operational and clinical services. This is the volume engine, driven largely by retail national account customers.
- **Oncology & Multispecialty**: specialty drug distribution, group purchasing, infusion, the U.S. Oncology Network (physician-led community cancer practices), PRISM Vision (retina and ophthalmology), Sarah Cannon research, Ontada technology, cell and gene therapy services (InspiroGene), vaccines. Grown by acquisition in FY2026 (PRISM Vision, 80% for $875 million; Core Ventures / Florida Cancer Specialists, 70% for $2.5 billion).
- **Prescription Technology Solutions**: electronic prior authorization, price transparency, patient enrolment and adherence services, third-party logistics for biopharma.
- **Medical-Surgical Solutions**: supplies and lab products to non-acute providers (physician offices, surgery centres, nursing homes, home health). Management intends to separate it as an independent company; Apollo funds agreed to buy about 13% for about $1.25 billion of convertible preferred (announced 2026-04-20, subject to approvals).

The Norway operations were sold on 2026-01-30 for $821 million.

Customers are pharmacies, health systems, physician practices, biopharma manufacturers and governments. Segment-level revenue and profit numbers are not in the filing excerpt.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Scale. $403 billion revenue at a gross margin of 3.61% only works with a huge, automated distribution network; a new entrant cannot match the purchasing and logistics cost. The US market is a small oligopoly (McKesson is one of the big three; the competitor names are not in the filing excerpt).
- Regulatory licensing (DEA, FDA, state boards) and controlled-substance compliance are a barrier, though also a cost (see Risks).
- Returns: the screen shows ROIC of 2.98 (298%) and a quality score of 0.71. The ROIC figure is extreme because distribution runs on very little net invested capital (negative working capital style); treat it as proof of capital-light economics, not a literal return on a new project.
- Mix shift toward higher-value services: oncology practice management, specialty drugs, prescription technology. Gross profit grew 9% against revenue +12% in FY2026 (Item 7), and opex fell as a share of revenue (2.07% vs 2.48%).

Evidence against:
- Gross margin slipped 10 basis points to 3.61%; pricing power on the core product is thin (brand price decreases and generic conversion partly offset volume, Item 7).
- Customer concentration: growth was "primarily from retail national account customers" (Item 7). Share of revenue by customer is not in the filing excerpt.
- The moat is a scale moat in a low-margin product; it protects returns, but it does not give pricing power.

Net: a real but narrow moat, strongest in oncology and technology services.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap, and the memo should say so plainly. The stock is up 43% in 6 months, 24% in 1 year and 141% in 3 years, and sits 3% below its 5-year high. EV/EBIT of 20.6x is 10% above its own history (ev_ebit_vs_hist 1.104). The valuation score is only 0.51 (middling). The screen picked MCK for quality, low dilution and strong balance sheet, not for a discount.

What the market worries about, in the few places where it does:
1. Opioid litigation and regulation: a long-tail legal liability (Item 1A). Settlements are mostly known, the claims charge was only -$3 million net in FY2026 versus $108 million in FY2025. I think this worry is largely priced and mostly cash-immaterial against $5.7 billion FCF, but new state taxes or new suits can recur.
2. Policy risk on drug pricing, reimbursement and the generic/brand mix. Management says outcomes are "difficult to predict" (Item 7, Government Policies). The worry is plausible, and I cannot dismiss it.
3. Earnings quality and the Medical-Surgical separation: execution risk and stranded costs. The market currently seems to like the separation.

So the question is not "why is it cheap" but "is a 4.6% FCF yield enough for a business that grows revenue 12% but FCF about 6%?" My answer: it is fair, not a bargain.

Screen says cyclical_normalized = False. FCF margins are very stable (std 0.17 points), so no peak-year adjustment is needed on cash flow. I still use a normalized EBIT near $5.7 billion because of the Norway gain and LIFO credit (see Numbers).

## Disruption risk
Not mandatory (not software, payments or internet), but note the Prescription Technology Solutions and Ontada technology units face AI-tool and PBM-platform competition, and the 10-K flags AI governance and data-leak risk (Item 1A). Direct-to-patient and manufacturer-direct channels are a longer-term threat to distribution margins; the filing excerpt does not quantify it.

## Risks
Top 5 from Item 1A (my words, most important first):
1. **Opioid and controlled-substance litigation.** Many suits from states, counties, cities and private parties; consent decrees already bind how MCK distributes; outcomes can include damages and forced practice changes. Reputational damage continues.
2. **Healthcare regulation and fraud-and-abuse law.** Frequent rule changes, the Anti-Kickback Statute and qui tam actions can raise costs, squeeze margins, or in the worst case cost licences or Medicare/Medicaid participation. States may add taxes on opioid distribution.
3. **Loss of the right to buy, store or distribute drugs.** DEA, FDA, state pharmacy boards and CMS can sanction or withdraw permits; a single facility failure can disrupt operations.
4. **Impairment charges.** Large goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions ($3.4 billion spent in FY2026 alone on PRISM Vision and Core Ventures) can be written down if growth or reimbursement disappoints or if the stock falls for a long period. Amounts are not in the filing excerpt.
5. **Privacy, cybersecurity and AI law.** Rising compliance burden, fines and class actions, and data-misuse risk through AI tools.

Also relevant but cut off in the excerpt (Item 1A was truncated at 14,000 characters): customer concentration, supplier and competitive pressure, and economic risks (not in the filing excerpt). Beyond Item 1A: the Medical-Surgical separation (execution, costs and a $1.0 billion secured term loan raised at the subsidiary) and share-price risk after a big run.

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 4.6% on reported FCF ($5,719 million over a $124.4 billion market cap), 4.8% normalized (XBRL screen). Not cyclical, so the two are close.
- EV/EBIT: 20.6x reported, 22.6x normalized, against a 1.104 ratio to its own history, meaning the multiple is about 10% above the company's average (the history series is not in the filing excerpt).
- Balance sheet is not a constraint: net debt/EBITDA 0.59x, net debt/FCF 0.67x.
- What is priced in: at a 4.8% normalized FCF yield, with the buyback at roughly 4% of market cap per year ($4.8 billion over $124 billion) adding to per-share growth, the price already assumes FCF growth of at least the 6% historical rate and continued margin gains from oncology and technology mix. Revenue growth of 12% with FCF growth of 6% shows margins are not yet expanding on a cash basis. If EBIT growth stays at high single digit to low double digit and the multiple holds, a holder might earn roughly 4.8% yield plus 6 to 10% growth (about 11 to 15% a year); if the multiple slips back to its average (about 10% lower), that costs about 3% a year over 3 years. Reasonable but not compelling, and not a margin-of-safety entry.
- Momentum: mom_12_1 of +11.6%; momentum score 0.407 is the weakest part of the screen, odd given the price run (the screen's computation window is not in the filing excerpt). Volatility 32% a year; worst 5-year drawdown -27%.

## Thesis
- Durable cash machine: FCF of $5.7 billion, margin steady around 1.4 to 1.5%, with very low variance; it funds roughly $5 billion a year of buybacks and dividends.
- Per-share compounding: diluted shares -3% in a year, a new $5 billion authorization, EPS $38.38 (inflated by one-offs, but the trend is up).
- Mix shift to oncology, specialty and technology services should lift profit per dollar of revenue (opex ratio already down 41 basis points).
- Medical-Surgical separation, with Apollo as a validating outside investor, could unlock value and sharpen focus.
- Strong balance sheet (0.59x net debt/EBITDA) gives capacity for more deals and buybacks.

Calibration: the thesis is good but mostly in the price. This is a hold-on-the-radar, not a buy at 20.6x EBIT.

## What breaks the thesis
1. Gross margin below about 3.4% (from 3.61%) for two consecutive quarters, which would mean core distribution pricing is eroding.
2. FCF margin below about 1.2% (the average minus roughly 1.5 standard deviations is 1.23%) on a trailing-twelve-month basis, or FCF under $5 billion.
3. A new opioid or controlled-substance settlement or claims charge above roughly $1 billion, or a new state tax or consent-decree term that adds clear cost.
4. Loss or restriction of a major retail national account customer, or of a DEA or state licence at a major facility.
5. A goodwill impairment on the oncology acquisitions, or the Medical-Surgical separation or Apollo deal failing or being repriced.

Buy-side trigger to upgrade to BUY-candidate: a pullback to a normalized FCF yield above about 5.5% (roughly a price near $900 on the same FCF, my arithmetic) or EV/EBIT back to or below its own historical average (about 18.7x, derived from 20.64/1.104), with the signals above intact.
