# BBY (Best Buy Co., Inc.) paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Sources: the inputs file only (10-K accession 0000764478-26-000009, XBRL screen, price stats, headlines). The MD&A excerpt in the file is cut off after its first paragraph, so segment data, operating income, gross margin and guidance are "not in the filing excerpt". Research only; no orders.

## Verdict
**WATCH.** A solid, net-cash, cash-generative retailer, but the stock is up 41% in 6 months, trades about 46% above its own EV/EBIT history, and has thin margins with no visible moat; wait for a better entry or proof of margin gains.

## Business
Best Buy sells consumer electronics, appliances and related services to US consumers through stores, online, in-home services and a growing third-party Marketplace. It also earns from membership programs (My Best Buy Plus, My Best Buy Total) and a retail media network (Best Buy Ads) (10-K 0000764478-26-000009, Item 1A). Its three largest states by sales are California, Texas and Florida (Item 1A). It directly imports only about 1% to 3% of its assortment, but depends on vendors who source from China, Mexico and Southeast Asia (Item 1A). Segment revenue split: not in the filing excerpt. Revenue for the year to 2026-01-31 was $41.69bn (XBRL screen).

## Moat
Evidence for: a service layer (installation, repair, in-home advice, memberships) that pure online sellers do not match; scale with vendors; high returns on capital (ROIC 45.7%, ROE 36.1%, XBRL screen); net cash.
Evidence against: the company itself says its service-heavy model gives it a cost structure "higher than some of our competitors", causing margin pressure; it competes with e-commerce, mass retailers, carriers and its own vendors selling direct (Item 1A). Revenue has shrunk at 3.4% a year (rev_cagr, XBRL screen). The high ROIC partly reflects a small equity and asset base typical of a low-margin retailer, not pricing power. My view: a narrow or no moat. It is a well-run category leader in a shrinking, price-competitive niche.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not very cheap any more. At a 5.8% FCF yield it looks inexpensive in absolute terms, but the stock has risen 41% in 6 months and 43% in a year, and EV/EBIT is 1.46x its own history. What the market worried about, and I think was partly right to, is: (1) shrinking sales as consumers delay device upgrades; (2) tariffs and supply chain exposure to China and Taiwan, where Best Buy may have to absorb costs (Item 1A); (3) online and AI-driven shopping channels eroding traffic. The recent rally reflects relief: Q3 results beat EPS consensus and full-year guidance was raised (headlines, 2026-11-24 and 11-25). I think the worries about demand were overdone near the lows, but the worry about structurally thin margins is still valid.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = False in the inputs. Even so, this is a discretionary, replacement-cycle business. The normalized figures from the screen are the sensible reference: normalized FCF yield 4.77% and normalized EV/EBIT 14.49 (XBRL screen). Both are less attractive than the headline numbers, which suggests the latest year sits somewhat above the multi-year average (average FCF margin 2.5% vs 3.0% latest).

## Disruption risk
Retail electronics faces AI shopping agents and AI-driven search that could bypass its site, plus vendors and carriers selling direct (Item 1A explicitly names AI-driven search and shopping bots). Not mandatory for this sector, but real.

## Risks (top 5 from Item 1A, most important first)
1. Competition and cost structure: e-commerce, mass retailers, carriers and vendors selling direct, against a service-heavy cost base; price-conscious shoppers pressure margins.
2. Macro and consumer demand: discretionary purchases of electronics are sensitive to jobs, inflation, rates, housing and credit; consumers can delay upgrades.
3. Tariffs, trade policy and geopolitics: reliance on vendor imports from China, Mexico and Southeast Asia; Taiwan risk for chips and TV panels; possible Chinese rare earth export bans; Middle East shipping disruption. Ability to pass on price rises is limited.
4. Technology and product-cycle change: fast category change, AI in consumer products, excess inventory and heavy discounting if forecasts miss.
5. Catastrophic events and talent: weather in California, Texas and Florida, plus the need to attract and keep trained staff (wage pressure).
Cybersecurity, regulatory and financial risks exist in the full Item 1A but fall beyond the excerpt (cut at 14,000 characters).

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 5.85% headline, 4.77% normalized (XBRL screen). On the normalized figure, the stock is priced for roughly flat cash flows with modest growth.
- EV/EBIT: 15.08 now, 14.49 normalized, and 1.459x its own history (XBRL screen). That implies a historical level near 10x, so the multiple has expanded by about a half.
- What is priced in: a stabilization in sales and sustained margin gains. Revenue has been shrinking, so a 15x EBIT multiple already assumes recovery.
- Cross-check: one headline cites a 12-month analyst target of $94.62, about 2% below the price (TradingView, 2026-11-24); that is a single labelled headline, treat with caution. Other analysts raised forecasts after Q3 (Benzinga, 2026-11-25).
- Screen scores: composite 0.64, quality 0.73, balance 0.93, valuation only 0.42, momentum 0.75 (XBRL screen). Strong quality and balance sheet; valuation is the weak leg.
- Price: $102.58 on 2026-11-25; volatility 40% a year; drawdown from the 5-year high only -3%; worst 5-year drawdown -44% (price stats). There is little cushion, and a 44% drawdown has happened.

## Thesis
- A net-cash retailer producing about $1.26bn of FCF, with 6% net share count shrinkage supporting per-share value.
- Quality metrics are high (ROIC 45.7%) and FCF has grown 12% a year while revenue declined, so margins and cost control are working.
- Positive earnings momentum: EPS beat and raised full-year outlook in Q3 (headlines).
- Optional upside from higher-margin lines (membership, Marketplace, ads), though their size is not in the filing excerpt.
Against: much of this is now in the price. This is why the verdict is WATCH rather than BUY-candidate.

## What breaks the thesis
- Revenue falls year on year again in the next two reported quarters after the Q3 beat.
- FCF margin falls below the 5-year average of 2.5% in the next fiscal year.
- Operating margin (implied about 3.3%) drops below 3% for 2 quarters, for example due to unrecovered tariff costs.
- Net cash turns to net debt, or buybacks stop while the share count rises.
- Guidance is cut after the November raise.
Entry-point signal (would upgrade to BUY-candidate): EV/EBIT back near 12x or normalized FCF yield above 6.5%, with revenue stable.
