# ADBE (Adobe Inc.) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: the inputs file only (10-K 0000796343-26-000003 for FY ending 2025-11-28, XBRL screen, price stats, headlines). Research only; no orders.

## Verdict
**WATCH** (leaning BUY-candidate on price): a 10.9% FCF yield and 10.5x EV/EBIT for a 41% FCF-margin subscription business is very cheap, but the stock is down 66% from its 5y high with negative momentum and the AI-disruption question cannot be settled from the filing; wait for evidence that ARR growth holds.

## Business
Adobe sells subscription software. FY2025 revenue was $23.77B, 96% subscription (10-K 0000796343-26-000003, Item 7, Revenue table).
- **Digital Media** ($17.65B, 74% of revenue, +11%): Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Illustrator, Lightroom, Premiere Pro, After Effects), Acrobat, Express and Firefly. Customers are creative professionals, creators, business professionals and consumers. Digital Media ARR was $19.20B, +11.5%.
- **Digital Experience** ($5.86B, 25%, +9%): Analytics, Real-Time CDP, Experience Manager, Commerce, Marketo, GenStudio, aimed at marketers and brand managers. Subscription revenue was $5.41B, +11%.
- **Publishing and Advertising** ($256M, 1%, -7%): legacy products.
- By customer group, Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription revenue was $16.30B (+11%) and Business Professionals & Consumers was $6.50B (+15%, driven by Acrobat) (Item 7).
- Geography: Americas 59%, EMEA 27%, APAC 14% (Item 7).
- Total Adobe ARR was $25.20B at year end, +11.5%. Remaining performance obligations were $22.52B, +13% (Item 7).

## Moat
For:
- Workflow lock-in. Customers use several Adobe products together, and the filing stresses end-to-end workflows (Item 7).
- Scale and retention. 96% subscription revenue recognised ratably, with RPO of $22.5B.
- Returns on capital are high: ROIC 55%, ROE 61% (XBRL screen).
- FCF margin was stable: average 39%, std 2.8 points (XBRL screen).
- "Commercially safe" Firefly AI and integration across the Creative Cloud apps.

Against:
- The 10-K itself lists "limited barriers to entry", customer price sensitivity and "AI or cloud-native companies" as competitors (Item 1A). It says prompt-based creation and editing, document understanding and AI agents compete directly.
- Third-party models may be restricted from Adobe's use (Item 1A).
- Growth is steady but not accelerating. Revenue CAGR is 10.6% (XBRL screen) against ARR growth of 11.5%.

Assessment: the moat is real in professional workflows (file formats, plug-in ecosystem, enterprise licences). It is much weaker in the low-end consumer and creator tier, which Express and Firefly target. The filing gives no churn, seat or pricing data, so I cannot test it. Not in the filing excerpt.

## Numbers (FY ending 2025-11-28)
- Revenue: $23,769M, up from $21,505M and $19,409M, +11% (10-K 0000796343-26-000003, Item 7). 3-year CAGR is 10.6% (XBRL screen).
- Operating income: not stated in the excerpt (it is cut off before the line). Derived: revenue $23.77B minus cost of revenue $2.55B minus opex $12.51B gives about $8.7B, or about 36.6% operating margin (derived from 10-K Item 7 rounded figures). This is consistent with EV/EBIT of 10.5 and net debt/EBITDA of 0.08 (XBRL screen).
- Net income: $7.13B, +28% (10-K, Item 7). Operating cash flow was $10.03B, +25%. FY2024 was hit by the $1B Figma termination fee (Item 7).
- FCF: $9,852M; FCF margin 41.5%; 3-year FCF CAGR 10.1% (XBRL screen).
- Gross margin: (23,769 - 2,551) / 23,769 = 89.3% (derived from Item 7). Subscription cost of revenue rose 13% to $2.03B, including AI inferencing and hosting costs, against subscription revenue +12% (Item 7).
- Opex was "relatively flat" at $12.51B (Item 7), so operating leverage drove the profit growth.
- Net debt: net debt/FCF is 0.08 and net debt/EBITDA is 0.08 (XBRL screen), so roughly $0.8B (derived), effectively unlevered.
- Share count: dilution is -9.3%, meaning a net reduction from buybacks (XBRL screen). The share count itself is not in the excerpt. Market cap $90.6B at $232.83 implies about 389M shares (derived).

## Why is it cheap?
The market worry is simple: generative AI could turn Adobe's tools into a commodity. If anyone can make an image, video or marketing asset by typing a prompt, fewer people need Photoshop seats, and AI-native competitors could take creators and small businesses. The stock has fallen 27% in a year and 62% in three, and the worst 5y drawdown was -72% (price stats). A second worry is margin: AI inference costs rise in cost of revenue, and the 10-K says AI costs "could adversely impact our margins" (Item 1A).

Is the worry right? Partly. The reported numbers do not show damage yet: ARR +11.5%, RPO +13%, Acrobat and Business Professionals +15%, margins steady. Multiples have compressed far more than growth has slowed, so the price already assumes a lot of decline. But the fiscal 2025 data predate much of the AI competition, and the 10-K says nothing on net new seats, price per seat or churn. The worry about the long run (5-10 years) is legitimate and unproven either way. My view: the market is over-penalising a business still growing about 10%, but I cannot call the bottom, and momentum is poor (12-1 month momentum -22%, momentum score 0.08, XBRL screen).

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = False. The business is subscription-based and not cyclical. FCF margin std is 2.8 points, so the reported year is a fair base. Normalized FCF yield is 10.2% and normalized EV/EBIT is 11.2x (XBRL screen). The one-off to note: FY2024 FCF was reduced by the $1B Figma fee, so FY2025 growth in operating cash flow (+25%) overstates the trend.

## Disruption risk
High and central: generative and agentic AI tools (prompt-based creation, document AI, AI agents) could erode demand for creative and document seats, and the 10-K names them as competitors (Item 1A). It could also cut pricing power at the low end.

## Risks (top 5, most important first)
1. **AI-driven competition and substitution.** New AI-native entrants and large platforms may offer prompt-based creation, editing and document tools that are cheaper or free, pressuring renewals, upsell and price. Adobe says there is no assurance its AI products will be adopted or monetised (Item 1A).
2. **Margin squeeze from AI costs.** Inference and third-party model costs rise as AI features are scaled without a clear price increase. Subscription cost of revenue is already growing slightly faster than subscription revenue (Item 7, Item 1A).
3. **AI regulation and IP liability.** EU AI Act phases run through 2030, and US state laws are emerging. Third-party models may have been trained without a proper licence, which creates infringement claims and reputational harm (Item 1A).
4. **Brand and creative-community backlash.** Terms, AI policy or misuse of its tools can cause customer attrition and reputation damage (Item 1A).
5. **Acquisition risk.** Past deals (the Figma termination cost $1B) show M&A can fail or disrupt; integration and benefit risk persist (Item 1A, Item 7). The 10-K risk excerpt is cut off, so further risks (security, FX, tax and others) are not in the filing excerpt.

## Valuation
- FCF yield 10.9% (FCF $9.85B / market cap $90.6B); normalized 10.2% (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT 10.5x; normalized 11.2x. The screen puts the ratio versus its own history at 0.31, meaning the current multiple is about 31% of its historical level (XBRL screen, interpretation mine). The history itself is not in the inputs.
- What is priced in: with 10% FCF yield and only about 0.8B net debt, the market assumes FCF will shrink materially or growth will fall to roughly zero or negative over time. If FCF merely stays flat at $9.9B, the owner earns about 11% a year before buybacks. Buybacks have cut the share count, which adds to that return. If FCF grows 8% a year, the implied return is high-teens. The thesis does not need a re-rating, only that the decline does not happen.
- Valuation score is 0.92 (very cheap), composite 0.64 (XBRL screen).
- Caveat: a low multiple on a business facing technology disruption can be a value trap. Stock-based compensation is not in the excerpt, so true owner FCF may be lower than the reported figure. Not in the filing excerpt.

## Thesis
- Cheap on cash: about 10-11% FCF yield with near-zero net debt and 41% FCF margin.
- Fundamentals are intact: ARR +11.5%, RPO +13%, revenue +11%, net income +28%, flat opex gives operating leverage.
- Buybacks are shrinking the share count (-9.3%), which compounds returns at this price.
- Optionality from AI monetisation (Firefly, Acrobat AI Assistant, GenStudio, Acrobat Studio) if usage converts to paid.
- Stock is already down 66% from its high, so expectations are low.

Position sizing view: if it moved to BUY, a starter position well below the 8% cap (for example 2-3%) fits given 42% volatility and the unresolved AI risk.

## What breaks the thesis
- Total Adobe ARR growth falls below 8% for two consecutive quarters (it was 11.5% at FY2025 end).
- Digital Media ARR or RPO growth falls below 8%.
- Operating margin below about 33% (derived FY2025 level about 36.6%) or gross margin below 87% (FY2025 about 89.3%), from AI costs or price cuts.
- FCF margin below 35% for two quarters (FY2025 41.5%, average 39%).
- Management cuts subscription prices or bundles AI in at lower prices to defend share, or buybacks stop while the share count rises.
