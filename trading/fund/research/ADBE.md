Paper memo only. No orders. Sources: the inputs file only (10-K accession 0000796343-26-000003, fiscal year ended 2025-11-28; XBRL screen; price stats; labelled headlines). The latest 10-Q (0000796343-26-000156, period 2026-08-28) is listed but its contents are not in the excerpt, so fiscal 2026 trends are not in the filing excerpt.

## Verdict
**BUY-candidate (starter size only, well below the 8% cap).** A 10-11% FCF yield, 55% ROIC and 11% growth are priced as if the business is shrinking; the real risk is generative AI eroding the franchise, which the 10-K itself names, and I cannot rule it out from this file.

## Business
Adobe sells subscription software. Subscription is 96% of revenue ($22,904M of $23,769M) (10-K 0000796343-26-000003, Item 7, Revenue table). Segments (fiscal 2025 revenue, same table and Segment Information):
- **Digital Media, $17,649M (74%), +11%.** Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Illustrator, Lightroom, Premiere Pro, After Effects), Acrobat, Express, Firefly. Customers are creative professionals, creators, business professionals and consumers. Digital Media ARR $19.20B, +11.5% (10-K, Item 7).
- **Digital Experience, $5,864M (25%), +9%.** Analytics, Real-Time CDP, Experience Manager, Commerce, Marketo, Campaign, GenStudio. Customers are marketers and brand managers. Subscription revenue $5,409M, +11% (10-K, Item 7).
- **Publishing and Advertising, $256M (1%), -7%.** Legacy products (10-K, Item 7).

Total Adobe ARR was $25.20B at fiscal year end, +11.5%; remaining performance obligations were $22.52B, +13% (10-K, Item 7, Financial Performance Summary). By customer group, Business Professionals and Consumers (mainly Acrobat) subscription revenue grew 15% to $6,495M; Creative and Marketing Professionals grew 11% to $16,303M (10-K, Item 7). Geography: Americas 59%, EMEA 27%, APAC 14%.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Very high returns on capital: ROIC 55.5%, ROE 61.3% (XBRL screen).
- Stable cash margins: FCF margin 41.5% against a 3-year-style average of 39.0% with a standard deviation of only 2.8 points (XBRL screen). That is the signature of sticky, ratably recognized subscriptions.
- Workflow lock-in: the 10-K says customers often run multiple Adobe products across both segments, and that the offerings are integrated software plus cloud. File formats, plugins, training and team workflows are real switching costs (10-K, Item 7, Customer-Focused Strategy).
- Growth is not slowing yet: ARR +11.5%, RPO +13%, revenue CAGR 10.6% (10-K, Item 7; XBRL screen).
- Cost of subscription grew 13% against revenue growth of 12%, and operating expenses were flat, so AI inference costs have not yet hurt margins visibly (10-K, Item 7).

Evidence against:
- The 10-K lists "AI or cloud-native companies" and "new entrants" as competitors, notes "limited barriers to entry" and customer price sensitivity, and says competitors may prevent or limit Adobe's use of third-party models (10-K, Item 1A).
- The moat is largely workflow and habit. If prompt-based creation replaces tool-based editing, habit matters less.
- Acrobat and Express face a free-tier and AI-assistant world; headlines question whether the freemium strategy can win users (headline 2026-11-26).

Net: the moat is real today and visible in the numbers, but its durability against generative AI is the open question. I rate it wide-but-contested.

## Why is it cheap?
Price stats: last close $232.83; 1-year return -27%, 3-year -62%, drawdown from the 5-year high -66%, worst 5-year drawdown -72%, 12-1 month momentum -22%; volatility 42% a year (price stats; XBRL screen). The momentum score is 0.08 out of 1, while the valuation score is 0.92.

What the market is worried about, in plain words:
1. **AI will make creative tools and PDF tools cheaper or free.** If anyone can type a prompt and get an image, video or document edit, why pay about $60 a month for a suite? Adobe's own 10-K says this is a risk (Item 1A).
2. **Seats shrink.** AI may reduce the number of designers and marketers, which are Adobe's customers (10-K, Item 1A: "AI solutions may continue to modify workforce needs").
3. **AI costs eat margin.** Inference is costlier than classic software (10-K, Item 1A and Item 7, cost of subscription).
4. **Past M&A.** The $1B Figma termination fee in fiscal 2024 reminds investors that big deals can fail (10-K, Item 7).

Is the worry right? Partly. The fear is about the terminal value, not the next two years: ARR, RPO and revenue are all still growing 11-13%, and margins are rising. A stock that falls 62% in 3 years while earnings grow is a re-rating, not an earnings collapse. But markets that price a risk this hard are sometimes right about the direction. I think the pricing is too harsh for a company with 41% FCF margins, but not absurdly so. The proof will be fiscal 2026 ARR growth, which is not in this file.

## Disruption risk
High and the central debate: generative and agentic AI (prompt-based creation and editing, AI document assistants, AI ad creation) from model labs and cloud-native entrants could displace Adobe's tool-based workflows and pressure pricing (10-K, Item 1A).

## Risks (top 5 from Item 1A, most important first)
1. **AI competition and failure to innovate.** Rivals may build better or faster AI, with different training data strategies or proprietary data, and win adoption. Adobe says there is no assurance its AI products will be adopted or monetized. This is the thesis-defining risk.
2. **Pricing and share pressure from low barriers to entry.** New entrants, short product cycles and price-sensitive customers may push down prices and margins and hurt renewal, upsell and cross-sell rates.
3. **AI cost and margin drag.** Compute for AI is costly, may rise, and Adobe may have trouble accessing models, datasets or hardware. Third-party model providers might also cut off access. Margins could suffer without matching adoption.
4. **AI regulation, IP and legal liability.** The EU AI Act applies to some operations with phased implementation through 2030; third-party models may have been trained without proper licenses, creating infringement exposure; fines and compliance costs are possible.
5. **Brand and creative-community backlash, and M&A execution.** Positions on AI, terms of service or content misuse could drive customer attrition. Acquisitions may fail to deliver benefits (the Figma deal ended with a $1B termination fee).

Item 1A excerpt was cut off at 14,000 characters; further risks (security, macro, FX, tax, and so on) are not in the filing excerpt.

## Valuation
- **FCF yield:** 10.87%; normalized 10.22% (XBRL screen). FCF $9,852M on market cap $90.62B.
- **EV/EBIT:** 10.5; normalized 11.24 (XBRL screen).
- **Versus own history:** ev_ebit_vs_hist = 0.3056 (XBRL screen); I read this as the current multiple being roughly 31% of its own historical level, or in the cheapest part of its range. The precise definition is not in the filing excerpt, so I treat it as "far below its own history", not as a precise number.
- **What is priced in:** at a 10% FCF yield, a no-growth perpetuity roughly equals the current price at a 10% discount rate. In other words the market assumes near zero long-term growth, or a decline, despite 10% FCF growth historically. With 9% share-count shrinkage at this yield, buybacks alone could add several percent a year to per-share value.
- **Sanity case (mine, illustrative):** if FCF only grows 3-5% a year and the multiple stays near 10x FCF, the return is roughly the yield plus growth, about 13-15% a year before any re-rating. If AI cuts FCF 5% a year for a decade, the return is about the yield less 5%, around 5%, still not a catastrophe at today's price. The downside is a faster collapse than that.
- Valuation score 0.92 (XBRL screen) is the best of the sub-scores; balance 0.85, quality 0.80, dilution 0.78.

## Thesis
- Cash machine at a distressed price: 41% FCF margin, 55% ROIC and almost no net debt, yet a 10-11% FCF yield.
- Growth is intact: ARR +11.5%, RPO +13%, revenue +11%, with opex flat so operating leverage is showing (10-K, Item 7).
- Capital return: the share count is shrinking about 9% (XBRL screen), and the FCF can fund that and more.
- Asymmetric: the price already assumes near-zero growth, so even modest AI success (Firefly, Acrobat AI Assistant, GenStudio) yields upside, and the 3-year -62% fall leaves a low bar.
- Embedded product base: Creative Cloud Pro, Acrobat and Experience Platform are workflow products with high switching costs and are adding AI rather than being replaced by it so far.

## What breaks the thesis
Checkable signals (the first three can be tested against the 10-Q for the period ended 2026-08-28 and the Q4 fiscal 2026 report that the headlines say is imminent):
1. **Total Adobe ARR growth below 8% year over year** at the fiscal 2026 year end (it was 11.5%), or two consecutive quarters of decelerating ARR growth.
2. **Digital Media ARR growth under 8%**, or net new Digital Media ARR falling year over year.
3. **FCF margin below 35%** for two quarters or fiscal year (it was 41.5%; its standard deviation is only 2.8 points), or cost of subscription revenue growing faster than 15% a year with no revenue acceleration.
4. **Share count stops shrinking** (dilution turns positive) because buybacks are replaced by large acquisitions, in particular a deal above about 10% of market cap.
5. **RPO growth falls below revenue growth** for two quarters (it was 13% against 11%), which would show bookings weakening before revenue.
