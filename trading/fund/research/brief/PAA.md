# PAA brief for the portfolio manager (2026-11-27)

**Verdict: WATCH.** High FCF yield (13.8%) and about 7% distribution yield, but after a +47% year, with unverified leverage and FCF that is shared with preferred and noncontrolling holders, there is no clear margin of safety.

## What it is
Crude-oil midstream: pipelines, storage, terminals and gathering (Permian focus) plus a big merchant crude marketing arm. FY2025 revenue $44.3B, of which only $1.76B is fee-based services; the rest is commodity resale at thin margin (10-K 0001070423-26-000014, Item 7). Selling its Canadian NGL business to Keyera (expected close around end of Q1 2026; status not in the excerpt).

## Key numbers (FY2025)
- Revenue $44,262M (XBRL screen); revenue CAGR -8.3%, driven by lower oil prices (NYMEX avg $65 vs $76).
- Operating income not reported as a line in the excerpt; my derivation about $1.38B (3.1% margin), approximate.
- Net income attributable to PAA $1,435M vs $772M; helped by a $213M swing in asset gains/impairments and lower tax (Item 7).
- FCF $2,293M, margin 5.2% (average 4.4%, sd 0.7 points), FCF CAGR +5.5% (XBRL screen).
- Interest expense $554M, up 29% (Item 7). Net debt in dollars: not in the filing excerpt. Screen ratios net_debt/EBITDA 0.10 look wrong given $3.0B of new notes in 2025; ignore and verify.
- Units 704M weighted vs 702M a year earlier; no buybacks (Item 5).
- Distribution $0.4175 per unit per quarter (about $1.67 a year, about 7.1% yield at $23.52), up from $0.3175 a year earlier (Item 5).
- Market cap $16.65B; FCF yield 13.8% (normalized 11.7%); EV/EBIT 11.8 (normalized 15.4); history comparison not available (XBRL screen).

## Why it is cheap
Oil-price and volume exposure, Permian contract resets to market (Item 7), higher debt costs after the EPIC acquisition, and an MLP-style structure where preferred and NCI claims reduce FCF per common unit. A Bank of America Sell rating on 26 Nov and a -10% month add sentiment pressure. I think the structure and contract-reset worries are partly right, so the 13.8% yield overstates the bargain.

## Cyclical check
Flag is False, so no normalization beyond the screen. FY2025 was a good earnings year, not a trough. My stress case (my assumption): FCF -25% to $1.72B still yields about 10% on the market cap before NCI and preferred.

## Thesis (for a later BUY)
- Growing fee-based income (+4%) covering a distribution raised repeatedly.
- $2.3B FCF on a $16.7B cap gives room for distribution growth and deleveraging, plus disposal proceeds.
- Strategy shift to core US crude lowers commodity sensitivity.
- Stated framework: more distributions, disciplined capex, investment-grade credit.
- Beat the Alerian Midstream index over five years (320.92 vs 291.01).

## What breaks the thesis
- Net debt/adjusted EBITDA above about 4x in a 10-Q, or loss of investment-grade rating.
- Distribution cut or frozen for two quarters.
- Fee (services) revenue down year on year for two quarters, or more contract resets below market.
- Annual FCF below about $1.7B or FCF margin sustained below 3.5%.
- Canadian NGL sale fails or proceeds not applied to debt/distributions.

## Risks
Crude price/volume; leverage and refinancing; contract repricing; execution on the disposal and acquisitions; cyber/operational events (Item 1A excerpt covers cybersecurity only).

## Headlines (30 days)
Mostly option-chain pages. Substantive: BofA Sell rating (26 Nov, negative), Q3 coverage of the Canadian NGL sale and "Silver Creek Deal" via PAGP (neutral), and a "has the stock run ahead of earnings" piece. Q3 2026 10-Q figures are not in the filing excerpt.

## What to do
Keep on the watchlist. Revisit after verifying net debt and coverage (distributions plus preferred plus NCI) from the 10-Q, or on a pullback that lifts normalized FCF yield well above 12% with leverage confirmed.
