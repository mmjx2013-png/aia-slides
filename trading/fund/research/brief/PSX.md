# PSX (Phillips 66): brief for the portfolio manager, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH. A reasonable integrated downstream business, but after +82% in one year the normalized FCF yield is only about 6.4% and rests on refining margins we cannot forecast. Not cheap anymore.

## Business
Integrated downstream: Midstream, Chemicals (a joint venture), Refining, Marketing and Specialties, and Renewable Fuels. Total assets are $73.7 billion (10-K 0001534701-26-000006, Item 7). Segment splits are not in the filing excerpt. No real moat: refining is a commodity business. Scale, integration and midstream fee income soften the cycle.

## Key numbers (FY2025)
- Revenue $132.4 billion; revenue CAGR -8.0% (XBRL screen).
- Net income $4.4 billion; operating cash flow $5 billion; capex and investments $2.2 billion (10-K, Item 7).
- FCF $4.96 billion, 3.75% margin; average margin 4.45%; FCF CAGR -22.9% (XBRL screen).
- Operating income and EV/EBIT: not in the filing excerpt. Net debt: not in the filing excerpt (the screen figure looks unreliable).
- 2025 payouts: $1.2 billion buybacks and $1.9 billion dividends (10-K, Item 7). Share count: screen dilution of -13.9%, which I read as a reduction but could not verify.
- Market cap $92.1 billion; price $236.08 (2026-11-25); 1m -12%, 1y +82%, 3y +120%; 20% below the 5-year high (XBRL screen, price stats).

## Why it is cheap (and why it no longer is)
FCF yield is 5.4%, or 6.4% normalized (XBRL screen). Worries: refining margins are cyclical and probably elevated, California exposure, the $833 million Propel Fuels judgment (appeal pending, 10% interest accruing; 10-K Item 3), and shrinking FCF. The Propel judgment is about 0.9% of market cap. The margin worry is the valid one. The stock last year was a buyback bargain at about $136 (Q4 2025 average, 10-K Item 5); at $236 that gap has closed.

## Cyclical check
Normalized FCF is about $5.9 billion (4.45% x revenue), which is the 6.4% yield. FY2025 is below that average, so it is not a clear peak year. A trough margin of 3% would imply about $4.0 billion FCF and a yield of about 4.3% on the current cap. Normalized EBIT is not available.

## Top risks
1. Refining margin cyclicality. 2. Operational and hazard events. 3. Legal: Propel, EPA consent decrees. 4. Renewable fuels policy and the Rodeo conversion. 5. Capital projects and joint ventures.

## Valuation
FCF yield 5.4% / 6.4% normalized. EV/EBIT versus history is not available. The price assumes margins stay near recent levels and buybacks continue.

## Thesis
- Scale integrated assets with midstream cushion.
- About 6.4% normalized FCF yield; payouts of 63% of FY2025 FCF.
- Pullback of 12% in one month may give a better entry.
- Legal matters bounded relative to market cap.
Why not a BUY-candidate: no margin edge, FCF trending down, key valuation inputs missing.

## What breaks the thesis
- Normalized FCF margin below about 3% on two annual filings.
- Operating cash flow below capex plus dividends for two quarters.
- Buybacks cut below $1.2 billion a year, or a dividend cut.
- A Propel appeal loss that raises the judgment materially above $833 million.
- Net debt rising materially once the level is confirmed.
- Renewable fuel credit or feedstock rule changes causing an impairment on the Rodeo Complex.
