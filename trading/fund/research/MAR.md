# MAR (Marriott International) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH. Excellent asset-light franchisor, but at about 2.4% FCF yield and 25.8x EV/EBIT (1.47x its own history) the price already assumes a lot; no margin of safety for a long-only fund.

## Business
Marriott is a worldwide franchisor, operator and licensor of hotels, residences and timeshare, under brands across price points (10-K 0001048286-26-000007, Item 7 Overview). It owns or leases very few hotels: 51 owned/leased of 9,805 properties at year-end 2025 (Item 7, Properties and Rooms). Customers are hotel owners (who pay fees) and, indirectly, travelers (business, group, leisure) who book through the Loyalty Program, direct channels and online intermediaries.

How it earns money (2025, $m, Item 7):
- Franchise fees 3,325 (+7%), including co-branded credit card and brand fees (+105 of the 212 increase), plus rooms growth (+94).
- Base management fees 1,322 (+3%); incentive management fees 791 (+3%). Gross fee revenue 5,438 (+5%); net fee revenue 5,303 after contract investment amortization of 135.
- Owned, leased and other revenue 1,679 less expense 1,461 = 218 net.
- Cost reimbursements: revenue 19,204 vs expense 19,503, net -299. This pass-through is most of reported revenue and has no profit meaning.

Reportable segments: U.S. & Canada (net fee revenue 2,921, segment profit 2,679), EMEA (621 / 525), Greater China (260 / 185), APEC (370 / 301). CALA sits in unallocated. Rooms: 1,779,936 (+4%), of which 1,183,513 franchised/licensed/other, 565,764 managed. Pipeline: about 4,100 properties and nearly 610,000 rooms, 43% under construction; 2026 net rooms growth guided to 4.5 to 5.0% (Item 7, System Growth and Pipeline).

## Moat
For:
- Scale and network effects: about 1.78m rooms, a Loyalty Program, direct digital channels and co-branded cards. Card and brand fees were the largest single driver of franchise fee growth in 2025.
- Owner economics: roughly 7,600 franchised properties pay percentage-of-revenue royalties with long contracts. Over 30% of 2025 signed rooms were conversions from competitor brands, which shows owners choosing the platform.
- Asset-light: capital and technology spend was $604m in 2025 against FCF of $2.6bn.
- Resilient fee stream: US RevPAR was +0.7% and worldwide +2.0%, yet net fee revenue grew 5%, because unit growth does most of the work.

Against / limits:
- Hotels are a competitive industry; owners can switch brands (the 10-K cites disputes and premature terminations, Item 1A).
- Intermediaries and AI planners could weaken direct loyalty (Item 1A).
- Select-service US demand was soft in 2025; US & Canada segment profit grew only 1%.
Net: a real moat in brand plus loyalty plus owner relationships, but it is a scale advantage, not a monopoly. Moat evidence is fair-to-strong, not unassailable.

## Numbers
Latest fiscal year 2025 (FY end 2025-12-31).
- Revenue: $26,186m (XBRL screen). Mostly reimbursed costs; net fee revenue $5,303m (10-K 0001048286-26-000007, Item 7, Fee Revenues).
- Operating income: not in the filing excerpt as a line item. Implied by the screen: EV/EBIT 25.77 (XBRL screen) with market cap $107.1bn and debt of $16.2bn gives EV of very roughly $120bn+, so EBIT of about $4.7bn to $5bn. This is my estimate, not a reported number; cash balance is not in the excerpt.
- FCF: $2,608m, FCF margin 9.96% of reported revenue (XBRL screen); five-year-style average margin 9.79% with standard deviation 1.24 points, so FCF is stable. On net fee revenue the margin would be about 49%, a more meaningful view (my calculation from the two figures above).
- Growth: revenue CAGR 8.0%, FCF CAGR 8.7% (XBRL screen).
- Net debt: debt $16,204m at year-end 2025, up $1,757m in the year (10-K, Item 7, Financing Activities). Cash not in the filing excerpt. The screen shows net_debt_fcf -0.1285 and net_debt_ebitda -0.0809, which would mean net cash. That is inconsistent with $16bn of debt and an interest bill of $809m (Item 7, Non-Operating), so I treat those two screen fields as unreliable (likely a data-tag artifact) and do not rely on them. Leverage is genuinely meaningful: single covenant of 4.5x maximum Adjusted Total Debt/EBITDA, satisfied (Item 7, Our Credit Facility). Weighted average rate 4.5%, 5.4 years maturity, 0.9 fixed share.
- Interest expense $809m, up from $695m (Item 7).
- Share count: dilution -0.1602 (XBRL screen; negative means a falling count, i.e. net buybacks). 2025 repurchases: 12.1m shares for $3.3bn; $350m more through 6 Feb 2026 (Item 7, Share Repurchases and Dividends). Dividends $0.63 then $0.67 a quarter.
- Capital returned ($3.3bn buyback plus dividends) exceeds FCF of $2.6bn, funded by new debt. That is the main quality caveat.
- Guided 2026 capex and other investments: $1.0 to 1.1bn, above 2025 (Item 7).
- ROIC and ROE: nan in the screen (not in the filing excerpt; equity is small or negative from buybacks).

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap. s_valuation is 0.21 (low score), FCF yield 2.4%, EV/EBIT 25.8 against a ratio-to-history of 1.47 (about 47% above its own average) (XBRL screen). The stock is up 41% in one year, 107% in three, and sits 3% below its five-year high.

What the market is (mildly) worried about, which keeps it from being even dearer:
1. US select-service softness, weak business transient and government travel (Item 7, Business Trends).
2. A slowing consumer and geopolitical or tariff shocks to travel.
3. AI and online intermediaries taking the customer relationship.
4. Leverage rising to fund buybacks.
I think the worries are modest and the premium multiple reflects quality and a buyback machine. The risk is that the price is the problem, not the business.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = False in the inputs, so no peak-year adjustment is applied. Still, lodging is demand-cyclical: fees fall with RevPAR (incentive fees especially; only 32% of US managed hotels earned them). Normalized FCF yield from the screen is 2.40% and normalized EV/EBIT 25.56, almost the same as the headline.

## Disruption risk
Moderate. The 10-K explicitly flags AI capabilities at intermediaries that may change how guests plan, book and pay, raising distribution costs and eroding loyalty (Item 1A). Not an existential threat to a fee model with 1.78m rooms, but a risk to the direct-booking and loyalty advantage that supports the multiple.

## Risks
1. Valuation and cycle sensitivity: a travel downturn (economic, geopolitical, health) cuts RevPAR and fees, hotel owners' returns and new-build starts (Item 1A, economic conditions), against a stock at 25x EBIT.
2. Leverage and buyback funding: debt rose $1.76bn to $16.2bn, buybacks exceeded FCF, interest cost rose 16%; covenant cap is 4.5x (Item 7).
3. Owner relationships: disputes and premature termination risk, more likely when hotel returns are weak; arbitration or litigation has occurred (Item 1A).
4. Distribution and AI: intermediaries, search and AI agents could raise costs and erode the loyalty edge; intermediary contracts renew every 2 to 3 years (Item 1A).
5. Regulation, IP and data: wide compliance exposure, plus the unresolved Starwood data incident for which total impact is not estimable (Item 7). Also Greater China RevPAR only +0.4%, and brand-fee reliance on co-branded cards.

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 2.44% headline, 2.40% normalized (XBRL screen). Dividends of about $2.64 a share are about 0.6% of the $415.77 price (my calculation).
- EV/EBIT: 25.77 (25.56 normalized), 1.465x its own historical level (XBRL screen).
- What is priced in: to earn a double-digit return from a 2.4% yield you need FCF growth near 8 to 9% a year (the past CAGR) for many years plus a stable multiple, or a continuing 3% net buyback. Unit growth of 4.5 to 5.0% plus modest RevPAR and card fees can deliver roughly 7 to 9% fee growth; that leaves little room for a multiple contraction. If the multiple reverts to its history, the stock could lose a third of its value even with good execution (illustrative, my estimate from the 1.47x ratio). A 31% worst five-year drawdown is already in the record; volatility is 27% a year.
- Headlines (below) include "may be 45% overvalued" and "looks fully valued"; I agree with the direction.

## Thesis (what would make it a buy)
- Compounding fee stream: 4.5 to 5% net rooms growth, pipeline of 610,000 rooms, 43% under construction.
- High-quality, asset-light cash flow with low volatility of FCF margin (std 1.24 points).
- Steady capital return: buybacks cut share count (dilution -16%).
- International and luxury strength offsets soft US select-service.
- A better entry price (EV/EBIT nearer its history, FCF yield 3.5%+) would turn this into a BUY-candidate.

## What breaks the thesis
- Worldwide RevPAR negative for 2 consecutive quarters (2025: +2.0%).
- Net rooms growth below 4% against the 4.5 to 5.0% guide.
- Debt growth continuing while FCF stalls: total debt above about $18bn without FCF above about $3bn.
- Interest expense above about $900m.
- Incentive fees share (69% of managed hotels) falling clearly, or franchise fee growth below 4%.
- Loss of a major owner or intermediary agreement; a cap on card fees or a loyalty program setback.
