# JBL (Jabil Inc) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: inputs file only. 10-K accession 0001628280-26-067038 (FY ended 2026-08-31, filed 2026-10-23). Paper research, no orders.

## Verdict
WATCH. A good, low-margin manufacturer with strong returns, but FY26 cash flow is flattered by supplier-payment timing, the AI-linked growth is concentrated, and the stock is not cheap against its own history (EV/EBIT about 1.39x its norm).

## Business
Jabil is a contract engineering and manufacturing services provider. It designs, builds, and ships products for other companies, and it buys the components itself (turnkey), so revenue is mostly pass-through materials plus a thin manufacturing margin (10-K 0001628280-26-067038, Item 7 Overview).

Three segments (10-K, Item 7, revenue mix FY26 / FY25 / FY24):
- Intelligent Infrastructure: 50% / 41% / 32%. AI and cloud data-center infrastructure, networking and communications, capital equipment. Revenue +45% in FY26, led by cloud and data center (+33% from existing customers).
- Regulated Industries: 35% / 40% / 42%. Automotive and transportation, healthcare and packaging, renewable and energy infrastructure. Revenue +7%.
- Connected Living and Digital Commerce (renamed Intelligent Devices and Robotics from 2026-09-01): 15% / 19% / 26%. Revenue -4%; the connected living business fell 11%.

Foreign-source revenue is 73.1% of the total (10-K, Item 7). Customer names and the concentration percentages are not in the filing excerpt.

## Moat
The evidence is mixed, and I lean towards "narrow".

For:
- ROIC of 35.9% and ROE of 64.6% (XBRL screen). ROE is inflated by buybacks and a thin equity base, so ROIC is the better figure.
- Scale and global footprint, plus integrated design, supply chain, and manufacturing, which make switching costly for a customer mid-program. This is my reading of the business description, not a measured fact.
- Management steers toward more complex, engineered work (the segment rename cites a "strategic shift towards more highly complex, engineered solutions").

Against:
- Gross margin is only 9.2% and operating margin 4.7% (10-K, Item 7, and my calculation of 1,704 / 35,954). That is the profile of a service with little pricing power.
- Customers "generally do not commit to long-term production schedules" and many do not commit beyond one quarter. They have cancelled orders and changed sourcing in the past (10-K, Item 1A).
- The filing names many direct competitors (diversified EMS and design providers). Operating income fell from 2,013 (FY24) to 1,182 (FY25), then recovered to 1,704 (10-K, Item 7), which shows the earnings are not steady.
- Item 1A notes that cloud customers have "different demand cycles", which breaks the historical pattern.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not clearly cheap, but the stock has fallen hard: -19% in 1 month, -37% in 6 months, -41% from its 5-year high, though still +16% over 1 year and +73% over 3 years (price pack). Volatility is about 50% a year.

What the market is probably worried about (my inference; the file has only headlines and the 10-K):
1. AI infrastructure demand is the growth engine (Intelligent Infrastructure is now half of revenue and grew 45%), and the stock trades as an AI-capex proxy. A slowdown in hyperscaler spending would hit both revenue and margin mix.
2. Baird cut its price target to $270 from $440 on 2026-11-23/24 (headlines). That is a 39% cut, which signals a sharp reset of expectations. I do not know the reason; it is not in the file.
3. The component shortage, notably memory, is called out in Item 1A as a current supply-chain constraint. Shortages can delay shipments and raise inventory.
4. Cash flow quality (the payables point above).

Is the worry right? Partly. Customer demand is genuinely uncommitted and concentrated, and the FY26 numbers are probably near a peak mix. But the balance sheet is sound, and the business still earns a high ROIC. On valuation, the stock is only "cheap" if FY26 earnings hold, and the screen says it is not cheap against its own past (see Valuation). The valuation sub-score is 0.42 on a 0-1 scale, below the quality sub-score of 0.74 (XBRL screen).

## Cyclical check (cyclical_normalized = True)
The inputs flag this name as cyclical, so I use the normalized case rather than the FY26 figures.
- Normalized FCF margin: 3.25% (5-year-style average, XBRL screen) vs 3.82% in FY26. On FY26 revenue, that is about $1,169M of FCF (my calculation: 3.252% x 35,954), against $1,374M reported. This matches a normalized FCF yield of 4.98% (XBRL screen).
- Normalized EV/EBIT: 14.18 vs 15.04 on FY26 (XBRL screen). The normalized EBIT is not stated in the file; I back out about $1.81B from EV (about $25.6B) at 14.18, which is above the FY26 figure of $1.70B. I treat this number with caution because the normalisation basis is not described.
- A harsher case: if operating margin reverted to the FY25 level of 4.0% on FY26 revenue, EBIT would be about $1.43B (my calculation), and EV/EBIT would be about 18x. If revenue fell as well, the multiple would be higher still. This is a stress scenario of mine, not a forecast.
- Margins are tied to utilization: Item 7 says operating margins improve at high volume and fall at low volume, and many costs are fixed (Item 1A).

## Disruption risk
Not mandatory for this name (hardware manufacturer). The nearest equivalents: AI-driven design changes at customers (for example, in-house or alternative sourcing by hyperscalers), and the risk of being displaced by competing manufacturers. Item 1A mentions the need to adopt machine learning and AI in operations to stay competitive.

## Risks
Top 5 from Item 1A, in my words, most important first:
1. Customers can cut, delay, or cancel at short notice. Few commit beyond one quarter, and the cost base is largely fixed, so a drop in orders hits margin quickly and can leave unsold inventory.
2. Customer concentration. A small number of customers account for a large share of revenue. Losing or shrinking one (or its financial failure) would hurt materially. This matters more as AI infrastructure grows to 50% of revenue.
3. Component supply and price. Single-source parts, current memory shortage, and the risk of pre-buying parts that become obsolete. Price pass-through is only periodic.
4. Working capital and credit exposure. Ramp-ups need cash before customers pay, and emerging customers get extended terms or loans. The FY26 payables stretch adds to this.
5. Technology and execution. Keeping pace with new processes, new business models, and fast growth, plus integration of acquisitions (Hanley, Rebound Technologies, Pii).

Also noted in Item 7: IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 2026-02-20 and refunds have started, with no material effect so far, but the tariff landscape "continues to shift rapidly".

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 5.85% on FY26 FCF; 4.98% normalized (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT: 15.04 on FY26; 14.18 normalized (XBRL screen). The ratio to its own history is 1.39 (ev_ebit_vs_hist, XBRL screen), which I read as the stock trading about 39% above its historical median multiple. The exact definition of the ratio is not in the file.
- Price: $228.14; market cap $23.49B (price pack, XBRL screen).
- What is priced in: a roughly 5% normalized FCF yield for a 3-4% margin business with a 1% long-run revenue CAGR means the market still expects the AI-driven step-up to persist, even after a 37% fall in six months. If FY26 margins are a peak, the current price leaves little margin of safety. If the AI mix proves durable (gross margin held near 9.2%), a 5-6% yield is fair, not cheap.
- The screens put quality at 0.74 and momentum at 0.81, valuation at 0.42, composite at 0.67 (XBRL screen).

## Thesis (what would make it a BUY)
- Intelligent Infrastructure keeps growing and holds gross margin near 9%, so FY26 earnings power is the new base, not the peak.
- Cash conversion holds when payables normalise: FCF stays above about $1.2B (about 3.3% margin).
- ROIC stays above 30% while the share count keeps falling.
- Regulated Industries (35% of revenue, +7%) offers a steadier base than the AI segment.
- A lower entry price would improve the odds. At about $228, the normalized FCF yield is under 5%.

## What breaks the thesis
1. Gross margin below 8.5% for two consecutive quarters (FY26: 9.2%).
2. Free cash flow below $1.0B on a trailing 12-month basis, or days in payables falling back toward 96 days with no offset from lower inventory.
3. Intelligent Infrastructure revenue growth turning negative year on year, or any disclosed loss of a top customer.
4. Days in inventory staying above 80 or rising, together with inventory write-downs.
5. Operating margin below 4.0% (the FY25 level) in any full fiscal year.
