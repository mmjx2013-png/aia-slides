# BBY (Best Buy Co., Inc.) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH. A solid, net-cash, buyback-shrinking retailer, but the stock is up 41% in 6 months to 102.58, trades at 1.38x its own EV/EBIT history, has flat sales, and sits above the one analyst target in the file (94.62). It is no longer cheap.

## Business
Best Buy sells consumer electronics, computing, mobile phones, appliances, gaming and related services to US and Canadian consumers, through 1,068 stores (926 Domestic, 142 International) and online (10-K 0000764478-26-000009, Item 7, Store Summary). Two reportable segments: Domestic (US plus Best Buy Health) and International (Canada).

Domestic revenue mix, FY2026: Computing and Mobile Phones 47%, Consumer Electronics 28%, Appliances 11%, Entertainment 7%, Services 6%, Other 1% (10-K, Item 7, Domestic segment). Domestic revenue was $38,278m and International $3,413m. Online was $13,166m, or 34.4% of Domestic revenue. The business is seasonal, with most profit in the holiday quarter. New profit streams are Best Buy Ads (retail media), Best Buy Marketplace (third-party sellers, launched FY2026) and memberships (My Best Buy Plus/Total). Best Buy Health is being restructured after impairments.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Scale as the largest specialty electronics retailer, with vendor partnerships and vendor co-funded store space (10-K, Item 7, strategy). The file gives no market share figure ("not in the filing excerpt"), though management says it "stabilized our market share position".
- Services and omnichannel: Geek Squad, in-home services and pickup. The 10-K itself says this service model gives a cost structure "higher than some of our competitors" (Item 1A).
- Very high ROIC of 89.5% and ROE of 36.1% (XBRL screen). ROIC looks inflated by a small invested capital base, so I would not treat it as proof of a moat.

Evidence against:
- Gross margin is flat at 22.5% to 22.6% across FY2025 and FY2026 and revenue is below FY2024 (10-K, Item 7). That is not pricing power.
- Vendors and carriers sell direct to consumers, and online and multi-channel rivals compete on fast, low-cost delivery (10-K, Item 1A).
- Product categories are exposed to vendor technology cycles and tariffs.

My read: a narrow, defensive moat (scale, service, vendor relationships), not a widening one. Margins show it holds position but does not earn excess returns on price.

## Why is it cheap?
Honestly, it is only modestly cheap now. The market worry is that consumer-electronics demand is stagnant and cyclical, with revenue 4% below its three-year-ago level. Other worries are tariffs on vendor-sourced goods, Amazon-style online competition, and AI shopping agents that could bypass the store and the brand. Appliances and home theater are still declining (-8.9% and -5.4% comps).

Is the worry right? Partly. Revenue growth is near zero and gross margin is flat. But the bear case has eased. Computing and phones (47% of Domestic) comped +5.7%, FY2026 comps turned positive, and the company is net cash with a 5.8% FCF yield. The 6-month rally of +41% shows the market has already moved from "melting ice cube" to "stabilized". The recent Q3 beat and raised full-year outlook (headlines) pushed it higher. The risk now is paying for the recovery after it has been priced.

## Disruption risk
Not mandatory (retail, not software or payments), but relevant: AI shopping agents and AI-driven search could redirect product discovery away from bestbuy.com. Management lists this as a risk and is partnering with platform providers on "agentic shopping" (10-K, Item 1A and Item 7).

## Risks
1. Consumer demand and the electronics replacement cycle. Discretionary, sensitive to rates, housing and confidence (10-K, Item 1A, Macroeconomic). The 5-year worst drawdown of -44% and 40% annual volatility reflect this.
2. Tariffs and supply chain. Almost all product is vendor-imported from China, Mexico and Southeast Asia. The Supreme Court voided IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20, but refunds are uncertain and new trade actions are possible (10-K, Item 7 and 1A). Taiwan or rare-earth disruption is also flagged.
3. Competition and margin pressure. Online rivals, vendors selling direct and carriers. Best Buy's service-heavy cost base is higher than some rivals (10-K, Item 1A). Marketplace also means competing with third-party sellers on its own site.
4. Technology and category cycles. Product life cycles, cannibalization and AI-driven product change could leave excess inventory or lost categories (10-K, Item 1A).
5. Execution on new profit streams and labor. Best Buy Health already produced $171m of impairments (Medicaid and Medicare Advantage pressures), restructuring charges are recurring, and Ads and Marketplace need investment. Labor cost and retention affect the service model (10-K, Item 7 and 1A).

## Valuation
- FCF yield 5.85% latest, 4.77% normalized (XBRL screen). Fair, not cheap, for a business with flat revenue. A 6% to 7% yield would be my bar for a clear BUY.
- EV/EBIT 14.2x latest and 13.7x normalized. The ev_ebit_vs_hist ratio is 1.377 (XBRL screen), which I read as the current multiple being about 38% above its own history. The method behind this ratio is "not in the filing excerpt", but the direction is clear: it is expensive against its own past.
- Valuation score is 0.43 versus quality 0.74 and momentum 0.75 (XBRL screen). The stock scores well on quality and momentum but only middling on value.
- What is priced in: continued comp stability, margin holding at about 3.3% GAAP (4.4% Domestic adjusted), buybacks, and a benign tariff outcome. The price is 102.58, 3% below the 5-year high, with total return of +43% in 1 year and +74% in 3 years. The headline price target of 94.62 implies 2% downside (TradingView, 2026-11-24). That is a single source and I do not rely on it, but it shows no obvious margin of safety.

## Thesis
- Net-cash balance sheet with falling share count and 5-6% FCF yield provides a floor and ongoing per-share growth.
- Comps have turned positive, led by computing and mobile, with a possible AI-PC and device refresh tailwind (my inference; not stated in the file).
- Mix shift to Ads, Marketplace and Services can lift margins without inventory (10-K, Item 7), though the evidence so far is small (Services comps +1.0%).
- Cost discipline: SG&A fell to 18.3% of revenue, with store and labor restructuring ongoing.
- Entry matters: a pullback toward a 6%+ normalized FCF yield (about $85 on the file's numbers, my arithmetic) would turn this into a BUY-candidate.

## What breaks the thesis
- Comparable sales negative for 2 consecutive quarters (they were +0.5% in FY2026).
- Domestic adjusted operating margin below 4.0% (4.4% in FY2026).
- Gross margin below 22.0% (22.5% in FY2026), for example from tariff cost absorption.
- FCF margin below 2.0%, versus 3.0% latest and a 2.5% average.
- Further restructuring or impairment charges above $200m in a year, or net cash turning into net debt.
