# NVDA brief for the portfolio manager (2026-11-27)

**Verdict: WATCH.** Exceptional business, cheapest on its own history, but a peak-cycle earnings base, heavy customer concentration and circular AI financing; no evidence in the inputs yet that the sell-off (-31% in 1 month) is overdone.

## Key numbers (FY ended 2026-01-25; 10-K 0001045810-26-000021, Item 7; XBRL screen)
- Revenue $215.9B (+65%); gross margin 71.1% (75.0% prior); operating income $130.4B (60.4% margin); EPS $4.90.
- FCF $96.7B (44.8% margin); multi-year average FCF margin 37.5%; normalized FCF about $80.9B (arithmetic).
- Net debt about zero per screen; share count shrinking (dilution -2.2%).
- Price $149.30, market cap $3.60T. FCF yield 2.69% reported, 2.25% normalized. EV/EBIT 27.6x reported, 34.6x normalized, about 0.6x its own history.
- Segments: Compute & Networking $193.5B (90%), Graphics $22.5B. Two direct customers are 22% and 14% of revenue.
- Price: 6m -30%, 1y -18%, 3y +197%, volatility 52%, 38% below the 5-year high.

## Why it is cheap
The market fears that AI data-center spending is a capex cycle that peaks; that NVIDIA is financing its own customers (illiquid stakes of $17.5B, $3.5B lease guarantees); that two customers dominate; that hyperscaler and rival chips take share; and that China sales stay near zero (H200 licensed but no revenue, 25% tariff). The first three worries are real and unanswered in the inputs. The speed of the fall looks partly like sentiment, but I cannot prove that. It is cheap versus its own history, not cheap in absolute terms (2.25% normalized FCF yield).

## Cyclical view
Underwrite normalized, not peak: roughly $81B FCF and about $104B EBIT. A harsher case (FCF margin one standard deviation, 13.5 points, below average) gives about $52B FCF, a 1.4% yield.

## Top risks
1. Customer financing and capex cycle, with two customers at 36% of revenue.
2. Circular investments and guarantees.
3. Product transitions, with inventory provisions of $7.2B in FY2026.
4. Export controls and tariffs.
5. Competition and open-source models.
(Item 1A excerpt in the inputs lacks the enumerated risks; ranking is my judgment from the Item 7 text.)

## Thesis
- 60% operating margin, 66% ROIC and a net-cash-like balance sheet give staying power.
- Full-stack systems plus networking (+142%) deepen the moat.
- Shrinking share count and 0.6x of historical EV/EBIT leave room if demand data hold.
- Sentiment is split, so a rebound is possible, but the margin of safety is thin on a normalized basis.

## What breaks the thesis (thresholds are my judgment)
- Gross margin below about 70% for two straight quarters ex one-offs.
- Data Center revenue down sequentially for two quarters.
- A top customer cutting orders, or write-downs or calls on the investments and guarantees.
- Provisions above about 4% of revenue, or a Rubin delay.
- Normalized FCF margin near 30% with the multiple still above 30x.

## Upgrade trigger
Latest 10-Q (period 2026-07-26; numbers not in the inputs) confirms growth and margins and the price falls further, or normalized FCF yield rises above about 3.5%.

## Headlines
Split and mostly low-information: negative notes on "five overhangs", a possible 1-2 quarter penalty box, market cap below Microsoft's, the Burry bear bet, and rival chip choices; positive notes on the cheapest valuation in ten years. No hard new data.

Position-size view: with a max of 8% per name and a stock with 52% volatility and a -66% worst drawdown, any starter position should be small until the 10-Q evidence is checked.
