# MAR brief for the portfolio manager (2026-11-27)

## Verdict
WATCH. High-quality asset-light franchisor, but 2.4% FCF yield and 25.8x EV/EBIT (1.47x its own history) leave no margin of safety. Revisit on a pullback.

## Key numbers (FY2025)
- Revenue $26,186m, mostly reimbursed costs; net fee revenue $5,303m, +5% (10-K 0001048286-26-000007, Item 7).
- FCF $2,608m, margin 9.96%, stable (std 1.24 points); revenue CAGR 8.0%, FCF CAGR 8.7% (XBRL screen).
- Operating income not in the excerpt; implied EBIT about $4.7 to 5bn from EV/EBIT 25.77 (my estimate).
- Debt $16.2bn, up $1.76bn; interest $809m, up 16%; leverage covenant 4.5x, satisfied. Screen net-debt ratios show net cash, contradicting this; ignored as a data artifact. Cash not in the excerpt.
- Buybacks $3.3bn (12.1m shares) plus dividends exceed FCF, funded by debt. Share count falling (dilution -16%).
- Price $415.77; 1y +41%, 3y +107%, 3% below 5y high; volatility 27%.
- System 9,805 hotels, 1.78m rooms; pipeline about 610,000 rooms (43% under construction); 2026 net rooms growth guide 4.5 to 5.0%.
- 2025 RevPAR worldwide +2.0%, US & Canada +0.7%, Greater China +0.4%.

## Why it is cheap (it is not)
Valuation score is low (0.21). The market has priced in quality and buybacks. Mild worries: soft US select-service and government travel, travel-cycle shocks, AI and online intermediaries, rising leverage. I think those worries are modest; the issue is price, not the business.

## Moat and disruption
Brand scale, Loyalty Program, co-branded cards, owner contracts, conversions (over 30% of signed rooms). Limits: owner disputes and terminations, intermediaries and AI planners (flagged in Item 1A). Disruption risk moderate. Not cyclical-normalized (flag False) but lodging is demand-sensitive; normalized FCF yield 2.40%, EV/EBIT 25.56.

## Top risks
1. Travel downturn at a 25x EBIT multiple.
2. Debt-funded buybacks and higher interest.
3. Owner disputes and terminations.
4. Distribution and AI disintermediation.
5. Regulation, data incident (impact not estimable), China weakness.

## Valuation
FCF yield 2.4%; dividend about 0.6%. Needs about 8 to 9% FCF growth and a steady multiple to earn a decent return; a reversion toward historical multiple could cost roughly a third of the price (illustrative).

## Thesis (for a later buy)
- Fee growth from 4.5 to 5% unit growth and a large pipeline.
- Asset-light, stable FCF.
- Continuous buybacks.
- International and luxury offset US select-service softness.
- Buy-candidate at a lower price (FCF yield 3.5%+ or EV/EBIT clearly nearer history).

## What breaks the thesis
- Worldwide RevPAR negative for 2 quarters.
- Net rooms growth below 4%.
- Debt above about $18bn without FCF above about $3bn.
- Interest expense above about $900m.
- Franchise fee growth below 4% or incentive fee share falling.
- Loss of a major owner or intermediary agreement, or a card-fee cap.

## Headlines (30 days)
Q3 beat, guidance raised, price targets lifted (Morgan Stanley, Bernstein). Valuation voices say "fully valued" and "45% overvalued". Pension buying, trivial insider sale. Q3 10-Q is not in the excerpt.
