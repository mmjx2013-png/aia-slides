# LRCX (Lam Research) - paper research memo, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH: a high-quality cyclical leader, down 42% in 6 months, but still 28x EBIT and a 2.1% FCF yield (2.7% normalized), so the price falls are not yet a margin of safety.

## Business
Lam sells wafer fabrication equipment (deposition, etch, clean) to chipmakers, plus a Customer Support Business Group (spares, upgrades, services, and the non-leading-edge Reliant line) (10-K 0000707549-26-000037, Item 7, Executive Summary).
- Customers: memory, foundry and integrated device manufacturers making NVM, DRAM and logic.
- Segments in FY26 (year ended 2026-06-28): Systems revenue $14.89B (+29.5%) and customer support-related revenue $8.35B (+20.2%) (10-K 0000707549-26-000037, Item 7, Revenue).
- Equipment and upgrade revenue by market: Foundry 54% (45% in FY25), Memory 39% (42%), Logic/IDM 7% (13%) (same section).
- Geography: China 34%, Taiwan 22%, Korea 19%, Japan 9%, US 7%, Southeast Asia 6%, Europe 3% (same section).
- Management says growth is driven by AI-related spending, 3D device architectures, multiple patterning and advanced packaging.

## Moat
For:
- Management cites technology leadership in deposition, etch and clean. It also cites a large installed base that feeds learning and spares, and close work with customers.
- The 10-K says customers qualify tools at a heavy cost and then tend to keep a supplier for that production-line application "for an extended period". That is a switching-cost moat, though it also locks Lam out where a rival won first.
- Returns are high: ROIC 61.0% and ROE 58.3% (XBRL screen). Customer support-related revenue is 36% of the total ($8.35B of $23.23B), a steadier stream than new tools (10-K, Item 7).
- Gross margin was 50.5%, up from 48.7% (10-K, Item 7, Gross Margin).

Against:
- Item 1A says competitors, including state-backed ones with larger resources, may "deeply discount or give away" similar products. New regional rivals are emerging.
- Customers have affiliated entities that build similar equipment.
- Rivals face fewer export-control limits for some applications.
- The Item 1A excerpt says customers may use AI to reduce their reliance on Lam for process development.
- Gross margin gains in FY26 came from "favorable customer mix", which is not a structural gain and can reverse.

Net view: a real but cyclical moat. Switching costs protect existing lines. The risk is regional competitors and policy, not a lack of technology.

## Numbers (FY26, year ended 2026-06-28)
- Revenue: $23.23B, +26.0% vs $18.44B (10-K 0000707549-26-000037, Item 7). The 3-year revenue CAGR is 10.0% (XBRL screen).
- Gross profit: $11.73B, 50.5% margin (10-K, Item 7).
- Operating income: about $8.20B (gross margin $11,725M less operating expenses $3,526M, my calculation from 10-K Item 7), a 35.3% operating margin. R&D was $2.38B (10.2% of revenue) and SG&A $1.15B (4.9%) (10-K, Item 7).
- Net income: $7.27B (+35.6%). Diluted EPS $5.76 vs $4.15 (10-K, Item 7). Effective tax rate 12.1% vs 10.1% (10-K, Item 7).
- Operating cash flow: $5.86B vs $6.17B in FY25, down despite 26% revenue growth. Capex $966M (10-K, Item 7, Executive Summary).
- FCF: $4.89B, a 21.05% margin (XBRL screen). The multi-year average FCF margin is 26.45% (std 3.2 points) and FCF CAGR is only 1.5% (XBRL screen), so cash flow has lagged earnings badly.
- Net debt: negative, i.e. net cash. Net debt/FCF is -0.38 and net debt/EBITDA -0.21 (XBRL screen), which implies roughly $1.9B net cash (my calculation). Cash and restricted cash were $5.60B (10-K, Item 7). $750M of notes were repaid in March 2026.
- Capital return in FY26: $3.85B buybacks (including net share settlement) and $1.27B dividends (10-K, Item 7), about $5.1B in total against $4.9B of FCF, so returns slightly exceeded FCF.
- Share count: the dilution measure is -7.16% (XBRL screen; negative means the count fell; the period is not in the filing excerpt). Buybacks are shrinking the share count.
- Deferred revenue fell to $2.43B from $2.68B on lower customer down payments (10-K, Item 7).
- Market cap $231.1B, EV/EBIT 27.96 (XBRL screen).

## Why is it cheap?
Honestly, it is not cheap on absolute numbers. It is cheaper than a few months ago: last close $185.01 on 2026-11-25, 1 month -23%, 6 months -42%, drawdown from the 5-year high -57%, annualized volatility 71% (price stats). The inputs do not state a cause for the fall. The labelled headlines are mostly positive and say demand is strong ("Equipment Demand Is Surging, But The Stock Is Down 52%", "The Price Has Caught Down The Story"). I therefore read it as a de-rating of a peak-looking cycle, not a business collapse. The likely worries, partly inferred, not stated in the file:
1. FY26 may be peak earnings: revenue +26%, with a 36% operating margin helped by mix.
2. China is 34% of revenue and exposed to export controls and tariffs (10-K, Item 7 and Item 1A).
3. Memory and foundry spending is timing-sensitive; the memory share fell 300 bps on "timing of customer investments".
4. A possible AI capex pause, which the 10-K flags with its warning on rapid growth and decline.
5. Cash conversion is weakening: FCF CAGR 1.5% vs revenue CAGR 10%.

Is the worry right? The cyclical worry is valid, and the stock still trades 33% above its own EV/EBIT history. A fall from the highs of a 3-year +164% run does not make it cheap. The Seeking Alpha title "Cheaper, Not Cheap" is a fair summary.

## Cyclical check (cyclical_normalized = True)
The screen's normalized figures use average margins, not the FY26 level:
- Normalized FCF yield 2.66% (vs 2.12% on FY26 FCF), implying normalized FCF of about $6.1B on the current market cap (my calculation). The normalized yield is higher because the average FCF margin (26.5%) exceeds FY26's (21.1%).
- Normalized EV/EBIT 31.4x (vs 28.0x on FY26) (XBRL screen). Normalized EBIT is therefore lower than FY26's $8.2B, about $7.3B (my calculation: EV of about $229B divided by 31.4). So FY26 operating income is above the screen's mid-cycle estimate.
- Two normalizations point in different directions (FCF higher, EBIT lower). That suggests the FY26 gap between EBIT and FCF reflects working capital and capex timing, which I cannot verify from the excerpt (cash flow statement not in the excerpt).

A mid-cycle case that does not rely on the peak: $6B FCF gives a 2.6% yield on $231B. This is a growth-priced stock, not a value one.

## Disruption risk
Not a software, payments or internet name. The relevant technology risk is the 10-K's warning that AI-based tools may reduce customers' reliance on Lam for process development and shrink its addressable market (10-K, Item 1A). Low-to-moderate.

## Risks (Item 1A, excerpt only)
1. **Cyclicality.** Demand swings rapidly with electronics demand, chip prices and customer plans. Cost structure is hard to resize on a multi-year R&D base, which can cut margins in a downturn. Most important, given the valuation.
2. **Trade restrictions, tariffs and China exposure.** China is 34% of FY26 revenue. MD&A says trade restrictions and tariffs "have had, and in the future may have" a negative effect on revenue and operating margin. Aluminum and steel tariffs already hurt gross margin.
3. **Competition, including state-backed rivals.** Larger-resourced, government-backed competitors may discount heavily, and some face no export-control limits. Customers may also build their own equipment.
4. **Technology shifts and AI.** A wrong bet on a technology path, product failure, or AI-driven process changes could leave obsolete inventory and a smaller addressable market.
5. **Supplier lock-in.** Once a customer picks a rival's tool for a production line, Lam rarely displaces it, so share is hard to win back.
The excerpt cut off at 14,000 characters, so supply chain, customer concentration and other later risks are "not in the filing excerpt".

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 2.12% on FY26 FCF, 2.66% normalized (XBRL screen). That is below a 10-year bond-style cash return, so the stock needs growth to pay.
- EV/EBIT: 27.96x on FY26 and 31.4x normalized; the ratio to its own history is 1.33, i.e. 33% above average (XBRL screen).
- Valuation sub-score is low (0.22), against momentum 0.89, quality 0.76 and balance 0.86 (XBRL screen). The composite is 0.676.
- Priced in: continued strong growth through the AI spending cycle, margins at or above the FY26 level, and no hard China restriction. A mid-cycle year with lower margins would put EV/EBIT well above 30x. Shareholder yield (buyback plus dividend, about 2.2% of market cap, my calculation from $5.1B) is the main cash return today.
- For comparison, the dilution trend and net cash are supportive, so balance-sheet risk is low.

## Thesis
- Leading deposition and etch franchise with switching costs and about 36% of revenue in customer support.
- Strong balance sheet (net cash), high ROIC (61%) and buybacks reducing the share count by about 7%.
- AI-driven 3D scaling and advanced packaging raise equipment intensity; memory makers are reported (headline) to be spending again.
- A 42% six-month fall is resetting expectations, so a better entry may appear if earnings hold.
- Position idea: watchlist only now. Revisit if EV/EBIT normalized falls toward 22-24x or FCF yield exceeds about 3.5% without a collapse in orders.

## What breaks the thesis
- Gross margin below 47% for two consecutive quarters (FY26: 50.5%).
- Quarterly revenue falling more than 15% sequentially, or a guide-down citing memory or foundry order delays.
- FCF margin below 15% on a trailing four-quarter basis (FY26: 21.1%), or operating cash flow staying below net income by more than $1.5B again (FY26: $5.86B vs $7.27B).
- China share of revenue falling below 25% because of new export controls, or a new rule that blocks servicing of installed tools there.
- Deferred revenue (customer down payments) below $2.0B (now $2.43B), signalling weaker order intake.
