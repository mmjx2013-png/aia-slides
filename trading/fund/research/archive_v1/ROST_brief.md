# ROST (Ross Stores) - PM brief, 2026-11-27

Source note: inputs file only. The Item 1A excerpt was a duplicate of MD&A, so the real risk-factor text and the Q3 10-Q are not in the filing excerpt.

## Verdict
**WATCH.** High-quality, net-cash off-price leader, but about 25x EV/EBIT (about 29% above its own history), FCF yield 2.5-3.1%, and capex rising to about $1.1B. Good business, thin margin of safety.

## Business
Largest US off-price apparel and home chain: Ross Dress for Less (1,904 stores) and dd's DISCOUNTS (363). Brand-name goods at 20-60% (dd's up to 70%) below regular prices, whole-family, value shopper. Mix: Home 26%, Ladies 22%, Men's 15%, Accessories etc. 15%, Shoes 13%, Children's 9% (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, Item 7). About 110 openings planned for 2026 (+5%).

## Key numbers (FY ended 2026-01-31)
- Revenue $22,751M, +8%; comps +5% (traffic +2%, basket +3%) (10-K, Item 7).
- Operating income $2,707M, margin 11.9% (FY24 12.2%) (10-K, Item 7). EPS $6.61 vs $6.32.
- CFO $3,027M, capex $819M, FCF $2,208M, FCF margin 9.7% vs 7.9% average (XBRL screen; 10-K, Item 7).
- Net cash: net debt/EBITDA -0.80 (XBRL screen); $4.6B cash vs $1.5B Senior Notes (10-K). Leases of $3.9B are outside this.
- Diluted shares down about 2% in FY25; $1,050M buyback, $528M dividends; new $2.55B buyback approved (10-K, Item 7).
- Valuation: FCF yield 3.10% (2.52% normalized), EV/EBIT 25.4x (1.29x own history), market cap $71.3B (XBRL screen). Price $223.26; 1y +29%, 3y +78%; worst 5y drawdown -38%.
- FCF quality: the $670M CFO jump leaned on timing (AP leverage 91% vs 87%, lower taxes and bonuses), so I treat FY25 FCF as somewhat flattered.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not. The market already pays a premium for steady comps, net cash and buybacks. Worries holding the multiple: tariffs (20 bp merchandise margin hit, about $0.16 EPS), the low-income consumer, and heavy investment (capex +34%, new distribution centers). The worries are fair for near-term margins and FCF; they do not threaten the franchise. The missing margin of safety is the issue.

## Moat
Scale buying and packaway inventory (37% of inventory), positive traffic, ROIC 59%, 11.9% operating margin. This is an operational moat, not a brand or network moat; rivals fish in the same pool of excess inventory. Competitor data is not in the filing excerpt.

## Main risks (from MD&A; Item 1A not in excerpt)
1. Valuation: about 34x trailing EPS leaves no room for a comp miss.
2. Tariffs and merchandise margin.
3. Consumer and macro, especially lower-income shoppers.
4. FY26 FCF pressure from capex, DC deleverage and possible reversal of the AP tailwind.
5. Fixed commitments ($3.9B leases, $5.0B purchase obligations) and new-market expansion.

## Thesis
- Positive-traffic comps from a buying-driven value model.
- Net cash and high returns fund growth, dividends and buybacks.
- 5% unit growth runway, with dd's re-accelerating.
- FY26 margin guided "slightly higher" than 11.9%.
- A better entry (lower multiple) would make this a BUY-candidate.

## What breaks the thesis
- Comps below 2% for two quarters, or negative traffic.
- Trailing operating margin below 11.0%, or guidance cut below FY25 margin.
- FCF margin below 6%, or AP leverage below 87%.
- Merchandise margin down more than 50 bps from tariffs with no offset.
- Net cash turning to net debt, or buybacks cut to fund capex.
