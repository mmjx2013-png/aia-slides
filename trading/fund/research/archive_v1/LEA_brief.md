# LEA (Lear Corp) - PM brief, 2026-11-27

## Verdict
WATCH: cheap (about 10.5% FCF yield, about 5x EV/EBIT, net cash), but thin margins, a 28% six-month drop the inputs do not explain, and 2026 trade and labor events keep it from BUY-candidate.

Derived items (operating income, EBITDA, net debt) are backed out of XBRL screen ratios because the file has no income statement. They are approximate.

## Business
Tier 1 auto supplier: Seating (seat systems, components) and E-Systems (wiring, high-voltage and low-voltage power distribution, controllers), sold to all major automakers (10-K 0000842162-26-000011, Item 7). Segment splits: not in the filing excerpt.

## Moat
Narrow. Life-of-program requirements contracts make revenue sticky, and ROIC is 15.2% (XBRL screen). But contracts carry annual price-downs, customers can in-source or direct suppliers, and Chinese suppliers accept lower returns (10-K, risk factors). FCF margin is a steady but thin 2.3% (XBRL screen). Five-year TSR is -19% against +96% for the S&P 500 (10-K, Item 5).

- Revenue $23.26B; FCF $527.2M; FCF margin 2.27% (XBRL screen).
- Market cap $5.01B; net cash about $1.0B (derived); EV about $4.0B (derived).
- EBIT about $777M, about 3.3% margin (derived); EBITDA about $1.38B (derived).

- Share count down about 10.6% over the screen window (XBRL screen, dilution -0.1056). $5.9B repurchased since 2011 at an average of $95.01; $775M authorization left, expiring 2026-12-31 (10-K, Item 5).
- Price $104.05; 6m -28%, 1y +2%; drawdown from 5y high -39% (price stats).
- Not in the filing excerpt: reported operating income, capex, gross debt, dividend, 2026 YTD results.

## Why is it cheap?
The market worries about thin, cyclical margins and a cluster of 2026 risks: tariffs on China, Mexico and Canada exposure; the USMCA review in 2026; union contracts covering about 67% of about 75,000 unionized workers expiring in 2026; slow EV adoption with program cancellations; Chinese competition; annual customer price-downs (10-K, risk factors). Management says tariff costs have been contractually recovered "to date". I think the worry is partly right: the risks are real and several resolve within a year. But a 0.48x-of-history EV/EBIT with net cash looks like it prices something close to margin collapse. The recent fall is unexplained because the Q3 2026 10-Q is not in the file. That is the key open item.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = True. Normalized FCF yield 10.6%, normalized EV/EBIT 4.84x (XBRL screen), implying normalized EBIT about $829M (derived). The FCF margin standard deviation is only 0.3 points, but the window may not include a deep recession. Stress case: FCF at half of normal (about $260M) is still about a 5% yield.

## Valuation
FCF yield 10.5% (10.6% normalized); EV/EBIT 5.16x (4.84x normalized), about half its own history (XBRL screen). Net cash is about 20% of market cap (derived). A 15% FCF haircut still leaves about a 9% yield.

## Top risks
1. Tariffs, USMCA review, trade policy.
2. Customer price-downs against cost-downs on a 3% margin.
3. Program and volume risk, including EV cancellations.
4. Labor disputes, with 2026 expiries.
5. Chinese competition and new entrants.

## Thesis
- FCF of $527M on a $5.0B cap; normalized yield 10.6%.
- Net cash about $1.0B; balance score 0.86 (XBRL screen).
- Buybacks have cut the share count about 10.6%; $775M authorization remains (renewal not confirmed).
- EV/EBIT about half its own history.
- ROIC 15.2% shows productive capital.

## What breaks the thesis
1. Adjusted EBIT margin below about 3.0% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Trailing four-quarter FCF below about $350M.
3. Net cash turning to net debt, or net debt/EBITDA above 0.5x.
4. A USMCA outcome adding non-recoverable tariffs, or customers refusing tariff reimbursement.
5. A strike at Lear or a major customer lasting more than a few weeks.
Also: buyback authorization not renewed after 2026-12-31.
