# MPC (Marathon Petroleum): PM brief, 2026-11-27

**Verdict: WATCH.** A good integrated refiner plus a large MPLX midstream stake, but up 85% in a year, cyclical cash flow, and the "cheap" case rests on a normalized FCF I cannot verify.

## Key numbers (FY2025; 10-K 0001510295-26-000009 and XBRL screen)
- Revenue $132.70B; FCF $4.77B (3.59% margin); 5-year average FCF margin 6.03%.
- Operating income not in the filing excerpt. Pre-tax income $7,015M (10-K Item 7), including $897M asset-sale gains, $253M legal settlements and $57M SRE; about $5.8B excluding them (my arithmetic).
- Diluted EPS $13.22 vs $10.08 in 2024 (10-K Item 7).
- ROIC 0.409, ROE 0.2337. Net debt/EBITDA -0.11 on the screen (net cash), which looks doubtful given $4.5B of new MPLX notes and interest cost up from $839M to $1,276M. Net debt in dollars is not in the excerpt.
- Share count falling fast on the screen (dilution -0.407); exact count not in the excerpt.
- Market cap $94.28B; $346.27 close on 2026-11-25; 1m -20%, 6m +39%, 1y +85%, 3y +146%; volatility 38%.
- MPC's MPLX stake: about 647M units, $34.55B at 12/31/2025, about 37% of today's market cap. MPLX distributions to MPC $2.56B in 2025.

## Valuation
- FCF yield 5.06% reported, 8.48% normalized (about $8.0B). My haircut case, $6.4B, gives about 6.8%.
- EV/EBIT 11.21 reported, 8.49 normalized. The screen's ev_ebit_vs_hist of 2.846 is undefined in the file.
- Caveat: consolidated FCF includes MPLX cash that partly belongs to outside unitholders, so yield on MPC market cap is flattered.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap on the screen's valuation score (0.448); the screen's momentum score is 0.975. It looks cheap only on normalized numbers. The market worries that 2025-26 margins are peak ("cheap on windfall earnings, fairly priced on 2028 earnings"). I think that worry is partly right. The 20% one-month drop has no company-specific bad news in the headlines; analysts are trimming targets (DBS to Hold, target cut to $460.38).

## Cyclical check
Normalized FCF about $8.0B vs $4.77B reported. The average probably includes super-cycle years, so I would not take it as mid-cycle. Sensitivity: $1.00/bbl blended crack is about $1,125M of annual R&M EBITDA; $1.00/bbl sour or sweet differential is $520M each (10-K Item 7).

## Business and moat
Refining & Marketing (2,986 mbpcd; adjusted EBITDA $6,138M), Midstream via MPLX ($6,750M), Renewable Diesel (-$110M). Moat is scale, crude flexibility and integration, not pricing power. Revenue CAGR -9.2% and FCF CAGR -30.1% (screen). No software or payments disruption risk; the slow risk is fuel demand erosion and low-carbon mandates.

## Top risks
1. Margin cyclicality. 2. Valuation after a large run. 3. California and RFS regulation costs (Los Angeles Rule 1109.1 through 2032). 4. Midstream leverage and acquisition returns ($4.5B notes; about $3.3B of deals). 5. Environmental liabilities (PFAS, HF alkylation, CERCLA).

## Thesis
- Integrated refiner, about 3.0 million bpd, advantaged crude slate.
- Midstream about half of segment EBITDA, distributions growing.
- High ROIC, heavy buybacks; normalized yields of 8.5% would be attractive if mid-cycle is near the average.
- The pullback may offer an entry, but it is not confirmed.

## What breaks the thesis
- Blended crack down more than $3/bbl vs 2025 for two quarters (about $3.4B of EBITDA at risk).
- FCF below about 4% of revenue for two more quarters with R&M EBITDA flat or down.
- Midstream EBITDA falls below $6,750M trailing, or MPLX leverage rises.
- Buybacks stop while the price is above $350.
- A final California or federal rule sharply raises Los Angeles costs, or the HF lawsuit succeeds.

Move to BUY-candidate only if the price falls to about $295 or lower (8% yield on $6.4B haircut FCF), or if two quarters confirm mid-cycle earnings above the haircut case. Not in the file: the Q3 2026 10-Q numbers, the share count, net debt in dollars, own-history EV/EBIT.
