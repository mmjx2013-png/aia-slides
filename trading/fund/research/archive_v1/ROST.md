## Verdict
**WATCH.** A high-quality, net-cash off-price retailer, but at about 25x EV/EBIT (about 29% above its own history) and a 2.5-3.1% FCF yield, the price already assumes steady execution, and a planned capex step-up to about $1.1B squeezes near-term FCF.

## Business
Ross operates two off-price apparel and home chains: Ross Dress for Less (1,904 stores in 44 states, DC, Guam, Puerto Rico) and dd's DISCOUNTS (363 stores in 22 states, a more moderately priced assortment). It sells first-quality, in-season brand-name apparel, accessories, footwear and home goods at 20-60% (Ross) or 20-70% (dd's) below department and specialty store prices (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, Item 7 Overview). It is the largest US off-price apparel and home chain (same source).

Customer: value-focused households, the whole family. Segments are not reported separately in the excerpt; the sales mix by category in fiscal 2025 was Home Accents and Bed and Bath 26%, Ladies 22%, Men's 15%, Accessories/Lingerie/Fine Jewelry/Cosmetics 15%, Shoes 13%, Children's 9% (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, Item 7, Sales).

Growth plan: 2,267 stores at fiscal 2025 year end (+4%), 90 openings in fiscal 2025, about 110 planned for 2026 (85 Ross, 25 dd's), 5% unit growth (Item 7, Store Openings). Selling square footage was 45.1M, up from 43.9M.

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Scale in opportunistic buying. Packaway inventory was 37% of total inventory (41% a year earlier), bought opportunistically and held under six months on average (Item 7, Operating Activities). This buying scale with brands and vendors is the core advantage, and it is hard for a small rival to copy.
- Consistent comps: +5%, +3%, +5% in fiscal 2025, 2024 and 2023, with traffic up about 2% and basket up about 3% in fiscal 2025 (Item 7, Sales). Positive traffic is good evidence the value proposition works.
- Returns: ROIC 59.2% and ROE 34.7% (XBRL screen). Very high, though ROIC on a lease-heavy retailer depends on how leases are treated, so treat it as directional.
- Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA -0.80 and net debt/FCF -1.17 (XBRL screen); $4.6B unrestricted cash against about $1.5B of Senior Notes, and an undrawn $1.3B revolver (Item 7, Liquidity).
- Operating margin 11.9%, very high for apparel retail (Item 7, Results of Operations).

Evidence against / limits:
- The moat is a buying and cost model, not a brand or network effect. Competitors (TJX, Burlington, and discount chains such as DG, which a headline compares) share the same supply of excess inventory. Competitor data is not in the filing excerpt.
- Margins are exposed to tariffs, which cut merchandise margin by 20 bps in fiscal 2025 (about $0.16 EPS) (Item 7, COGS and EPS).
- No pricing power evidence beyond comps; the model depends on low-income and value customer traffic.

Net: a real but operational moat (scale and buying discipline), not a durable-franchise moat.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap, and the memo should say so. The screen valuation score is 0.32 (low) and EV/EBIT is 25.4x vs 1.29x its own history. The stock is up 29% over 1 year and 78% over 3 years (price pack). It is a quality compounder that the market already pays for.

What the market worries about (and what holds the multiple back from going higher):
1. Tariffs: a 20 bp merchandise margin hit in FY25. Fair worry, but the company expects a recovery in FY26 margin. I think the risk is moderate and mostly in the price.
2. Consumer health at the low-to-middle income end. Comps were healthy, so no current evidence of damage, but the exposure is real.
3. Heavy investment: capex rising from $819M to about $1.1B (+34%), a new distribution center in Randleman NC and the Buckeye AZ DC deleveraging 25 bps. This lowers FCF in FY26 unless working capital helps. The worry is right for near-term FCF, wrong for long-term returns if new stores keep earning well.
4. Valuation itself. A headline says shares "could be 199% above fair value", which is a poor-quality, low-signal source; I discount it.

Fair summary: the stock is not mispriced cheap; the debate is whether the premium is deserved. I think quality is deserved, but the margin of safety is thin.

## Risks
The Item 1A text is not in the excerpt, so these are drawn from MD&A and are my reading, not the company's ranking.
1. Valuation and expectations risk (most important): 25x EV/EBIT and about 34x trailing EPS ($223.26 / $6.61) leave little room for a comp miss. A price-based risk, not from the filing.
2. Tariffs and merchandise margin: a $0.16 EPS hit already in FY25; further tariff action or vendor cost pass-through could cut the guided margin recovery (Item 7, EPS; Key Initiatives).
3. Consumer and macro: the filing says it continues to monitor tariffs, inflation and geopolitics (Item 7, Key Initiatives). Low-income shoppers are the exposed group.
4. Cost growth and capex: DC startup deleverage (25 bps in FY25), store-related costs for key initiatives, and capex rising to about $1.1B; FCF could fall in FY26 and the AP leverage tailwind could reverse (Item 7).
5. Fixed commitments and expansion execution: $3.9B operating leases, $1.1B NY ground lease, $5.0B purchase obligations; 5% unit growth into new markets (NY Metro, Puerto Rico) with dd's re-accelerating after only 10 openings in FY25 (Item 7, Contractual Obligations, Store Openings).

## Thesis
- Consistent, positive-traffic comps (+5% in FY25) from a buying-driven value model; the largest US off-price apparel and home chain.
- Net cash, low leverage, and a returns profile (ROIC 59%) that funds growth, dividends (raised about 10%) and buybacks (new $2.55B program) without debt.
- Unit growth runway: about 110 openings in 2026 (5%), with dd's re-accelerating and new markets.
- Margin recovery guided for FY26 ("slightly higher" than 11.9%) as DC deleverage fades and tariff costs are managed.
- Only as a WATCH: the thesis is sound but the entry price is the problem. A pullback to a lower multiple would improve the case.

## What breaks the thesis
- Comparable store sales below 2% for two consecutive quarters, or negative traffic.
- Operating margin below 11.0% on a trailing-12-month basis (vs 11.9% FY25), or FY26 guidance cut to a margin below FY25.
- FCF margin below 6% for the fiscal year (vs 9.7% FY25 and 7.9% average), or AP leverage falling below 87%.
- Merchandise margin down more than 50 bps from tariffs without offset.
- Net cash turning to net debt, or buybacks cut to fund capex.
