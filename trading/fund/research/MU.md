# MU, Micron Technology: paper research memo (2026-11-27)

## Verdict
WATCH. The stock looks very cheap on peak-year numbers (6.5x EV/EBIT, 8.7% FCF yield), but FY2026 is an extreme cycle peak (81% gross margin, revenue up 256%), and on normalized numbers it is expensive (28x EV/EBIT, 0.5% FCF yield). The new take-or-pay contracts might change the cycle, but that is unproven.

## Business
Micron makes DRAM and NAND memory and storage chips. It sells to data-center and cloud customers, PC and phone makers, and carmakers and industrial users. The 10-K reports four business units (10-K 0000723125-26-000023, Item 7, Revenue by Business Unit):

- CMBU (cloud memory): $43.1B, 32% of revenue, 72% operating margin.
- CDBU (core data center): $37.6B, 28%, 79% operating margin.
- MCBU (mobile and client): $36.6B, 27%, 80% operating margin.
- AEBU (automotive and embedded): $15.9B, 12%, 71% operating margin.

Fiscal 2026 had 53 weeks, which adds roughly 2% to the year. DRAM sales rose 252%, from about 180% higher average selling prices and mid-20% higher bit shipments. NAND sales rose 274%, from about 200% higher prices and mid-20% higher bits (Item 7, Total Revenue). Almost all of the growth is price, not volume. About one-third of revenue comes from customers headquartered outside the US, and about 70% ships to locations outside the US (Item 1A, international operations).

## Moat
Evidence for:
- Memory is an oligopoly with very high capital needs, and the filing says industry supply is constrained.
- Operating margin reached 75% and ROIC 74.6% (XBRL screen), which shows real pricing power in this upcycle.
- Take-or-pay strategic customer agreements began in Q3 and Q4 FY2026. They carry binding multi-year volumes, and most have price bands. Management says even floor-price gross margins would be "meaningfully above our peak quarterly margins in any past cycle" (Item 7, Strategic Customer Agreements). That is a claim from management, not a result.

Evidence against:
- DRAM and NAND are commodities. The filing says annual average selling prices over five years have ranged from about +200% to about -50% (Item 1A). Margins of 40% in FY2025 and 22% in FY2024 are the normal part of this range.
- The filing says high prices can lead customers to cut memory content, can push them to alternative technologies, and can attract new entrants (Item 1A).
- Micron's own margin in FY2024 was 5% operating, so the moat did not protect through the last downturn.

My view: there is a cost and technology moat against small players, but not against price swings. Returns on capital are a function of the cycle phase.

## Why is it cheap?
It is cheap only on the peak-year numbers. The market is pricing the numbers as non-recurring, and I think that is mostly right. The worries, in plain words:

1. Memory prices have risen about 180% to 200% in one year. Past cycles ended when new supply arrived and prices fell. The stock fell 35% in a month (price stats), and the headlines name a TrendForce cut to its DRAM price forecast, an analyst warning of a future supply glut, a UBS target cut, and a BNP Paribas downgrade.
2. New tariffs hit memory stocks (headline, 24/7 Wall St., 2026-11-25). The detail is not in the excerpt.
3. Spending is going up. A headline refers to a "surprising spending increase", which is not detailed in the excerpt. For a commodity maker, higher capex is how past gluts started.
4. Share dilution of 4.6% a year is a drag on per-share value.

Is the worry right? Partly. The 10-K itself says prices may fall "as we and others increase supply". The strategic agreements are a real counter, but they started only two quarters ago, most have price bands (so prices can still move within the band), and the excerpt does not say what share of volume they cover. I would not treat 6.5x EBIT as cheap until the share of contracted volume is known.

## Cyclical check
The inputs say cyclical_normalized = True, so the normalized case is the one that counts.

- Normalized FCF yield is 0.48% (XBRL screen), against 8.67% on the FY2026 number. That implies normalized FCF of about $3.3B on a $680B market cap (my arithmetic).
- Normalized EV/EBIT is 28.2x (XBRL screen), implying normalized EBIT of roughly $23B on an enterprise value of about $647B (my arithmetic). The FY2026 figure is $99.3B, so the screen treats about 77% of the FY2026 operating income as above-normal.
- The screen's normalization averages across the whole cycle, including the FY2023 trough. That is conservative if the take-or-pay contracts have really lifted the floor. It is fair if they have not.
- FY2025 is a middle-of-cycle reference point: 40% gross margin, $9.8B operating income. At FY2025 EBIT, EV/EBIT would be about 66x (my arithmetic). Even if margins settled between FY2025 and FY2026, the stock is not obviously cheap.
- Current EV/EBIT of 6.5x is 0.17 of the stock's own history on the screen's ratio (ev_ebit_vs_hist = 0.1747). The low ratio is the signature of a peak-earnings trap as often as of a bargain.

## Disruption risk
Not a software, payments or internet name. The relevant AI risk is demand-side: the 10-K says AI demand timing and size "can change significantly" and Micron has "incurred and expect[s] to continue to incur costs in anticipation of demand that ultimately may not materialize" (Item 1A).

## Risks
Ordered by importance, from Item 1A and Item 7:

1. Price collapse. The five-year range of average selling prices is +200% to -50%. Prices sometimes fall below manufacturing cost. With much of the cost base fixed, a drop would hit margins fast.
2. AI demand and capex overbuild. AI demand is hard to forecast. Micron and its rivals are raising supply and spending, and customers may have overstated demand to secure supply (Item 1A).
3. Demand destruction and new entrants. High prices can push customers to use less memory or alternatives, and can bring in new competitors.
4. Geopolitics and trade. Operations are in Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, Malaysia, China and India. Tariffs and export controls could cut access to customers or raise costs. The headlines mention new tariffs this week.
5. Execution on nodes and capacity. Node transitions, new fabs and yields can cause inventory build-up, under-used capacity and write-downs. The 10-K also flags customer concentration, power and water supply, and tax change in Singapore (the Pillar Two rule already cut the incentive benefit).

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 8.67% on FY2026 FCF, 0.48% normalized (XBRL screen). The normalized number is the one to use.
- EV/EBIT: 6.5x now against 28.2x normalized (XBRL screen).
- What is priced in: at $601 (market cap about $680B) the market is paying 28x normalized EBIT. That only works if the contract-driven margins hold well above old cycles, or if the market believes earnings will stay well above $23B. It does not require a repeat of FY2026, but it does need the new floor to be real.
- Momentum: 12-1 month momentum is +315% (screen) and the 3-year return is +672%, but the last month was -35% and drawdown from the 5y high is -50%. Volatility is 87% a year. A position here has to be sized for 50% to 60% drawdowns, which have already happened (worst 5y drawdown -58%).
- Sizing: the fund's maximum is 8% per name. With this volatility, 8% implies a roughly 4-5% portfolio hit on a 55% fall in the stock, which is tolerable, but I would start well below the cap if this became a BUY-candidate.

## Thesis
What would have to be true for a long position (not currently met in full):
- The strategic take-or-pay agreements cover a large share of volume, with price floors high enough that gross margin stays well above 40% through a downturn.
- AI memory demand (HBM, data-center DRAM and SSD) keeps outgrowing supply for several more quarters.
- Operating income stays above about $40B a year, which would put EV/EBIT near 16x.
- Cash generation funds capex, buybacks and dividends without more dilution. The balance sheet is a net cash position.

## What breaks the thesis
Checkable signals:
- Quarterly gross margin below 60% for two quarters (the FY2026 level was 81%).
- Management says capex or capacity additions rise and industry supply growth is above demand growth. A concrete test is next quarter's guidance on capex and bit shipments.
- Contract prices: DRAM or NAND contract price declines in two consecutive months in TrendForce data, or a strategic agreement renegotiated at the band floor.
- Disclosure that strategic agreements cover less than about one-third of volume, or that volume commitments were cut.
- FCF margin below 25% for two quarters, or share count growth above 5% a year.
- Tariff or export rules that cut shipments to a major customer region.

## If AI data-centre capex falls 30% next year
Customer concentration: not in the filing excerpt (the Item 1A section is cut off before it). Segment data: CMBU plus CDBU are $80.7B, 60% of FY2026 revenue (Item 7), which I treat as the exposed share. Not all of it is AI capex-driven.
- Base case, volume only: 30% x $80.7B = about $24.2B lost, 18% of revenue, leaving about $109B. COGS ($25.7B) is largely fixed (Item 1A), so gross profit falls to about $83B, a 76% margin. Operating income is about $75B.
- Harsh case, prices fall twice as hard: 60% x $80.7B = about $48.4B lost, revenue about $85B, gross profit about $59B (70% margin), operating income about $51B.
- FCF: capex is not in the excerpt, so I assume it is unchanged. Base: $59.0B less $24.2B x (1 - 14.8% tax) = about $38B. Harsh: about $18B. FCF stays positive.
- Caveat: these margins are still near peak, so the real risk is a larger price drop than this. FY2025 gross margin was 40%.
