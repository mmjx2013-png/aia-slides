# MA (Mastercard Incorporated): paper research memo

Date of inputs: 2026-11-27. Last close 659.97 (2026-11-25). Paper research only; no orders.
Source tags: (10-K 0001141391-26-000013, section) = FY2025 10-K filed 2026-02-11; (XBRL screen) = screen numbers in the inputs file. Items marked "derived" are my arithmetic on those figures.

## Verdict
**WATCH.** One of the best businesses in the universe (57.6% operating margin, ~87% ROIC, shrinking share count), but at 32x EV/EBIT and a 2.9% FCF yield, with the stock near its 5-year high, the price already pays for years of double-digit growth. The case is a quality-at-a-fair-price idea that needs a pullback or evidence of a faster compounding rate before it is a BUY-candidate.

## Business
Mastercard describes itself as "a technology company in the global payments industry" (10-K, Item 7, Business Overview). It runs a proprietary global network that switches (authorizes, clears, settles) card transactions, plus ACH/account-to-account capabilities. It sells to issuing banks, acquirers/merchants, governments, digital partners and businesses. It is not a bank: it does not issue cards, extend credit or earn interest, and does not set interchange or acquirer fees (10-K, Item 7).

Revenue comes from two blocks (the split between them is not in the filing excerpt):
- Payment network assessments (excerpt, 10-K Item 7, Key Metrics). These are gross standard-price charges before rebates and incentives, so they do not add up to net revenue:
  - Domestic assessments $11,029m, +8%.
  - Cross-border assessments $12,021m, +18%.
  - Transaction processing assessments $15,930m, +17%.
  - Other network assessments $1,018m, +9%.
- Value-added services and solutions: security, consumer acquisition and engagement, insights, digital and authentication, processing and gateway. The amount is not in the filing excerpt.

Volume drivers in 2025 (10-K, Item 7, Key Drivers):
- Gross dollar volume +9% in USD terms. US +6%, rest of world +10%.
- Cross-border volume +18% in USD terms (+15% local currency).
- Switched transactions +10%.

## Moat
Evidence for a wide moat:
- **Margins and returns.** GAAP operating margin 57.6%, adjusted 59.2% (10-K, Item 7). ROIC 0.87 and FCF margin 52% with a standard deviation of only 2.3 points over the screen window (XBRL screen). Margins this high and stable mean customers have little pricing alternative.
- **Network effect and standards.** The franchise model sets the rules that make issuers, acquirers and merchants interoperable, and the filing says the capabilities are "fundamentally interdependent" (10-K, Item 7).
- **Growth in line with volume plus pricing and services.** Revenue +16% against GDV +9% and transactions +10% means revenue is growing faster than volume. The excess comes from cross-border, processing and value-added services. Cross-border is the highest-yielding piece and is the most exposed to travel and FX cycles.
- **Capital-light.** Net debt/EBITDA is 0.47 (XBRL screen).

Evidence against, or limits:
- Mastercard is half of a duopoly with a larger rival, which is not in the file. The 10-K excerpt is silent on market share, so I make no share claim.
- Regulators are circling the moat itself: network fee reviews, interchange caps, no-surcharge rules and sovereign networks (see Risks). A moat that regulators can narrow by statute is real but not impregnable.
- Account-to-account and national schemes are explicitly named as alternatives (10-K, Item 1A).

Net: strong moat, with the main threat being politics and new rails rather than a competing card brand.

Observations:
- Buybacks of $11.7bn plus dividends of $2.8bn equal $14.5bn, about 85% of FCF, so most of the cash goes back to holders. The buyback retires about 2% of shares a year at current prices.
- The tax rate rose 3.8 points in 2025, which cost about 3 points of net income growth. Revenue +16% became net income +16% and adjusted EPS +17%. Further rate increases are not signalled in the excerpt.
- Litigation provisions of $504m in 2025, $680m in 2024 and $539m in 2023 are recurring, not truly one-off (10-K, Item 7, Non-GAAP). I treat about $0.5bn a year as a normal cost of business.
- Latest 10-Q (period 2026-09-30, filed 2026-10-29) exists, but its numbers are not in the inputs. Current-year trends are not in the filing excerpt.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap. The composite screen is 0.64, driven by quality (0.81), growth (0.78) and balance sheet (0.73); the valuation sub-score is only 0.40 (XBRL screen). The stock is up 23% in 1 year, 34% in 6 months and 8% in the last month, and sits 2% below its 5-year high (price stats).

What the market has worried about, and what could bring it back:
1. **Stablecoins and new payment rails** (a Motley Fool headline on 2026-11-25 asks if they threaten the networks). The worry is that cheaper rails take transaction volume. I think the threat is real at the margin, mostly in cross-border and B2B flows, but it is slow, and Mastercard is itself adding ACH and account-to-account capabilities (10-K, Item 7).
2. **Regulation and litigation**: interchange, surcharging, network fee reviews. This is the worry I consider most credible, because it hits pricing power, not volume.
3. **Consumer slowdown**: US GDV growth was only +6%.

So the "worry" now is mainly valuation, not fundamentals. The tape suggests the market is currently rotating into payment networks as a defensive-growth place (TradingView, 2026-11-26), which can reverse.

## Cyclical check
cyclical_normalized = False (XBRL screen). Payments volume is tied to consumer spending and cross-border travel, but FCF margin has been stable (std 2.3 points). The normalized figures are close to the reported ones: normalized FCF yield 2.71% versus 2.87% reported, and normalized EV/EBIT 33.1x versus 32.1x (XBRL screen). No peak-year adjustment is needed. A 2020-style travel shock is not in the file and would hit cross-border (about 37% of gross assessments, derived: 12,021/39,998) hardest.

## Disruption risk (payments)
Stablecoins, real-time account-to-account payments, wallets that route over other rails, and AI agents that choose a payment method are the credible disruptors; Mastercard's AI-commerce partnership headlines (Debia, 2026-11-26) suggest it is trying to be the rail for agentic payments, but the result is unproven and the 10-K excerpt gives no revenue figure for it.

## Risks (top 5 from Item 1A, most important first)
1. **Payments regulation and network-fee scrutiny.** Regulators have expanded their authority; several jurisdictions are looking at the fees Mastercard charges its customers, which could lead to direct regulation of network fees. Debit rules could spread to credit. Systemic-importance designations add compliance cost (10-K, Item 1A). This threatens the pricing that produces the margin.
2. **Interchange and surcharging.** Mastercard does not earn interchange, but interchange affects acceptance and issuer promotion. If interchange is cut, issuers may reduce rewards or demand fee cuts from Mastercard; if it is too high, merchants route away. No-surcharge rules now permit US and Canadian merchants to surcharge credit cards, which could push consumers to other payment means. Fines and civil damages are possible, and have been recurring ($504m in 2025).
3. **Protective government actions and local networks.** Governments mandate domestic switching, data localization and sovereign schemes, and sanctions and geopolitics can shut markets. This can remove entire geographies from Mastercard's switching and weakens growth outside the US.
4. **Privacy, data, AI and cybersecurity regulation.** Fragmented, conflicting rules (EU AI Act, data transfer bans, forced data sharing) raise costs and fines and could limit data-driven services (10-K, Item 1A). The excerpt was cut off before the competition and technology section, so the stablecoin and rail threat is covered above from the headlines rather than from the 10-K.
5. **Valuation-sensitive concentration of cash flow in cross-border and a macro downturn.** This is my addition, since the Item 1A excerpt stopped at about 14,000 characters; the economy and settlement-risk sections are not in the filing excerpt.

## Valuation
- Market cap about $598bn; FCF $17.2bn gives an FCF yield of 2.87% (2.71% normalized) (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT 32.1x, 1.10x its own history (normalized 33.1x) (XBRL screen). The stock is about 10% richer than its own average multiple.
- With buybacks at about 2% of the share count and revenue growing at the low-to-mid teens, an owner earns roughly: 2.9% FCF yield + low-teens FCF growth. If the multiple stays flat, that is an annual return of about low-to-mid teens; if it reverts to its own historical multiple, it falls by roughly one to two points a year over five years (my estimate, derived).
- What is priced in: continued 12-15% revenue growth, stable 57-59% margins and no punitive regulation for many years. There is little margin of safety. Against the fund's purpose (long-term holdings, no leverage), the position is good to own at a lower price, not at this price.
- Analyst targets in the headlines are not reliable: one source writes "$77" and another "$770" for the same Tigress target, and a third quotes $751 (Quiver). These are marketing-grade and I do not use them.

## Thesis (what a BUY would need)
- Compounding machine: revenue CAGR 13.8%, FCF CAGR 16.9%, FCF margin 49-52% (XBRL screen).
- Revenue growing faster than volume (16% vs GDV 9%) through cross-border, processing and value-added services (10-K, Item 7).
- Shrinking share count: diluted shares 946m to 906m in two years, buybacks about 70% of FCF.
- Near-zero financial risk: net debt/EBITDA 0.47x.
- Quality screen at the top of the range (ROIC 87%), so a 15-20% pullback would make this a plausible BUY.

## What breaks the thesis
- Revenue growth (currency-neutral) below 10% for two consecutive quarters (10-Qs).
- Adjusted operating margin below 57% (it was 59.2% in FY2025).
- Cross-border volume growth below 10% for two quarters, against +15% local currency in 2025.
- A binding rule, law or settlement that cuts network fees or interchange in the US, UK or EU, or removes no-surcharge rules.
- Effective tax rate above 22% on a sustained basis (it was 19.4%).
- Valuation entry signal rather than a thesis breaker: FCF yield above 3.5%, or EV/EBIT below about 26x (derived, about 20% below the current level), would move this to BUY-candidate.
