# LRCX (Lam Research): paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: inputs file only. Latest 10-K accession 0000707549-26-000037 (FY ended 2026-06-28). Price 185.01 on 2026-11-25.

## Verdict
WATCH: a high-quality, cyclical toll-road on AI chip capex, but after a 42% six-month drop it still trades at about 28x EBIT and a 2.1% FCF yield (2.7% normalized), so there is no margin of safety for a cyclical.

## Business
Lam sells wafer fabrication equipment (deposition, etch, clean) to chipmakers, plus a Customer Support Business Group that sells services, spares, upgrades and older "Reliant" tools (10-K 0000707549-26-000037, Item 7).
- Systems revenue FY26: $14.89B (+29.5%). Customer support-related and other: $8.35B (+20.2%), about 36% of revenue (Item 7).
- End markets by equipment revenue: Foundry 54% (45% a year earlier), Memory 39% (42%), Logic/IDM 7% (13%) (Item 7).
- Geography: China 34%, Taiwan 22%, Korea 19%, Japan 9%, US 7%, SE Asia 6%, Europe 3% (Item 7).
- Customers are a handful of leading memory, foundry and IDM makers (Item 7).

## Moat
For:
- Installed-base lock-in. Once a customer qualifies a supplier on a production line it generally keeps buying that supplier's tools for that application (Item 1A). This works for Lam too, and it is also the risk when a rival wins a line.
- Recurring customer support revenue of $8.35B (+20%) is a less cyclical layer on top of the systems business (Item 7).
- Position in deposition, etch and clean at the 3D device scaling inflection (3D architecture, multiple patterning, advanced packaging) (Item 7).
- Financial proof: gross margin 50.5% (+180 bps) and R&D of $2.38B, 10.2% of revenue (Item 7). Sustained R&D and margin at this scale are hard for new entrants to match.
- ROIC fraction 0.61 and ROE 0.58 (XBRL screen).
Against:
- China is 34% of revenue and there is competition from "regional competitors" and state-backed rivals that may discount tools deeply or sell where Lam is barred by export controls (Item 1A).
- Customers can build their own equipment affiliates, and AI may reduce reliance on Lam for process development (Item 1A).
- The moat protects share, not through-cycle pricing. FCF margin ranged around a 26.5% average with a 3.2-point standard deviation (XBRL screen), and the latest year was below that average.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap in absolute terms, only cheaper than it was. The stock is down 23% in a month, 42% in six months and 57% from its 5-year high, yet up 24% over one year and 164% over three (price stats).
What the market is worried about:
1. An AI-capex peak. Headlines on 2026-11-25 tie a 10% one-day fall to an AI and tech selloff and fears of new China curbs. The fear is that the AI-driven wafer-equipment boom is borrowing from future years.
2. China exposure of 34% of revenue and export controls (Item 7, Item 1A).
3. Expected analyst estimate cuts (one 2026-11-25 headline: "Expecting Street Cuts").
4. Quality of growth: FY26 growth was led by foundry, including mature-node spending, and the memory mix fell on "timing of customer investments" (Item 7). Mature-node spending is the type most likely to fade.
My view: the worries are partly right. Equipment demand follows capex, and capex follows chipmaker profits, which are cyclical. But the drop mostly resets a very rich multiple. EV/EBIT is 28.0x, still 1.33x its own history, so the derating has not yet gone below normal. Selling off 42% does not make a 2.1% FCF yield cheap.

## Cyclical check (cyclical_normalized = True)
- Normalized FCF uses the average FCF margin of 26.45% on current revenue, giving about $6.1B. Normalized FCF yield is 2.66% (XBRL screen) against 2.12% on actual FCF.
- Normalized EV/EBIT is 31.4x vs 28.0x on actual (XBRL screen). Normalizing makes the stock look more expensive on EBIT, which means FY26 operating margin of 35% is not clearly above its own average. On FCF, it makes the stock look somewhat cheaper because FY26 FCF margin was below average.
- The two signals conflict. I weight the EBIT view more, because FCF was depressed by working capital and capex, which may reverse, but I cannot verify that from the excerpt.
- Neither measure gives a mid-cycle yield above 3%. That is a thin return for a name that fell 58% in its worst drawdown in 5 years and runs at 71% annual volatility.

## Disruption risk
The 10-K names AI as both a demand driver and a threat: AI-based process development could shrink Lam's addressable market or reduce customers' reliance on it (Item 1A). Not a software or payments name; I rate this a medium risk, secondary to the cycle.

## Risks (top 5, most important first)
1. Cyclical capex downturn. Demand has "historically been characterized by rapid changes"; the company has to size its supply chain and staff through booms and busts, and a downturn would hit systems revenue ($14.9B) hardest (Item 1A).
2. China and export controls. 34% of revenue is China, rivals not bound by US controls can sell where Lam cannot, and policy shifts can remove revenue quickly (Item 7, Item 1A). The 2026-11-25 headline cites China curb fears.
3. Customer concentration and competition. A few chipmakers make up the market; losing a qualification on one production line locks Lam out for years. State-backed and regional rivals may discount heavily (Item 1A).
4. Technology and AI-driven change. Lithography-led scaling may be reaching its limit, and Lam must pick the correct technology path. AI tools could reduce customers' need for Lam's process development (Item 1A).
5. Valuation and expectations risk, not from Item 1A but from the numbers: 28x EBIT and 2.1% FCF yield leave little room for an estimate cut, and tariffs on aluminum and steel already weigh on gross margin (Item 7). The Item 1A excerpt was cut off, so later risks (supply chain, tariffs, litigation) are not in the filing excerpt.

## Valuation
- FCF yield 2.12%, normalized 2.66% (XBRL screen). Market cap $231B (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT 27.96x, normalized 31.43x, and 1.33x its own history (XBRL screen), so the multiple is about 33% above its own norm. Implied own-history EV/EBIT is about 21x (derived).
- Trailing P/E about 32x ($185.01 / $5.76, derived).
- Valuation score is 0.21, while momentum score (0.89) and balance sheet score (0.93) are strong (XBRL screen). The composite 0.69 is carried by quality and balance sheet, not price.
- What is priced in: FCF would have to grow roughly 8-10% a year for years to earn a double-digit return from a 2.7% starting yield, and FY26 earned only 1.5% FCF CAGR. The price assumes AI-driven spending stays elevated through the next cycle and that China is not shut. At 21x EBIT (its own norm) and unchanged EBIT, the stock would be about 25% lower; on $8.2B EBIT that is about $172B EV (derived, rough).

## Thesis (if it were to become a candidate)
- Lam is a top-tier owner of the deposition and etch share needed for 3D NAND, DRAM and advanced packaging, with a growing, stickier $8.3B service layer.
- Net cash, 35% operating margin and $5B of annual capital return give it resilience in a downturn.
- AI-driven wafer fab spending is rising across both memory and non-memory (Item 7), and memory makers are spending again (2026-11-26 headline).
- The derating (-42% in 6 months) is moving toward an acceptable entry; a further 20-25% fall would put it near its own historical EV/EBIT.
- Position sizing: if bought at all, a starter position well below the 8% cap given 71% volatility.

## What breaks the thesis
- Gross margin below 48% for 2 consecutive quarters (FY26: 50.5%; FY25: 48.7%).
- Revenue per quarter falling more than 15% sequentially, or the next 10-Q/10-K showing systems revenue below customer support revenue growth for 2 quarters.
- China share above 40% of revenue, or a new export rule that forces Lam to stop shipping to a major China customer.
- Deferred revenue (customer down payments) falling below $2.0B (FY26: $2.43B, falling), a sign that customers are cutting orders.
- FCF below $4B on a trailing twelve month basis, or buybacks plus dividends above FCF for a second year (FY26: $5.12B vs $4.89B).
