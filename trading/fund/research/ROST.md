# ROST (Ross Stores, Inc.): paper research memo, 2026-11-27

Source: inputs file only. Sources are cited as (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, section) or (XBRL screen). Fiscal 2025 = year ended 2026-01-31.

## Verdict
WATCH. This is a high-quality, net-cash off-price retailer, but at about 25x EV/EBIT and a 3.1% FCF yield, with the stock up 29% in a year, the price already pays for the quality. Nothing in the file shows a margin of safety.

## Business
- Ross operates two off-price apparel and home chains: Ross Dress for Less (1,904 stores in 44 states, DC, Guam and Puerto Rico) and dd's DISCOUNTS (363 stores in 22 states), 2,267 stores in total at 2026-01-31 (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, Item 7 Overview, Store Openings).
- It sells first-quality, in-season brand-name and designer goods at 20-60% below department and specialty store prices (dd's: 20-70% below moderate department and discount store prices). Customers are value-seeking households, with dd's aimed at a more moderate-income shopper (Item 7 Overview).
- Sales mix for fiscal 2025: Home Accents and Bed and Bath 26%, Ladies 22%, Men's 15%, Accessories/Lingerie/Fine Jewelry/Cosmetics 15%, Shoes 13%, Children's 9% (Item 7, Sales).
- Stores only; no e-commerce (Item 1A, Competition).
- Segments: the excerpt does not break out segment financials ("not in the filing excerpt"). The two banners are not separately reported here.
- Growth plan: about 110 new stores in 2026 (85 Ross, 25 dd's), about 5% unit growth. 90 stores were opened in fiscal 2025, including entry into Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area (Item 7, Store Openings).

## Moat
For:
- Scale. It is the largest US off-price apparel and home chain (Item 7 Overview). Buying scale gives it priority access to closeout and excess inventory.
- Opportunistic buying, packaway inventory (37% of inventory, down from 41%) and fast turns let it hold a price gap to full-price retailers (Item 7, Operating Activities; Item 1A, Strategic Risks).
- Returns are high: ROIC 59% and ROE 35% (XBRL screen).
- Operating margin is stable at 11.3-12.2% over three years, and free cash flow margin has a standard deviation of only 1.5 points (Item 7, Results of Operations; XBRL screen).
- Customer traffic is positive: fiscal 2025 comparable sales rose 5%, from about 3% basket and 2% traffic (Item 7, Sales).

Against:
- The filing itself says "there are limited economic barriers for others to enter the off-price retail sector" (Item 1A, Competition). The moat is scale and buyer relationships, not a legal or network barrier.
- The supply of closeout goods depends on vendors. If vendors manage inventory better, supply shrinks (Item 1A, Strategic Risks).
- Over half of goods originate from China (Item 1A).
- Newer markets may be less profitable than mature ones.

Net: a real but operational moat, narrower than a brand or network moat. It is evidenced by returns and margin stability rather than by pricing power.

## Numbers (fiscal 2025, year ended 2026-01-31)
- Revenue: $22,751M, up 8% from $21,129M. Comparable sales +5% (10-K 0000745732-26-000006, Item 7, Highlights). XBRL revenue $22,750.6M (XBRL screen).
- Operating income: $2,707M, 11.9% margin, versus $2,586M and 12.2% in fiscal 2024. Fiscal 2024 included a $61.6M gain on a warehouse sale, so the decline in margin is partly a comparison effect (Item 7, Highlights, SG&A).
- Net income: $2,145M. Diluted EPS $6.61 versus $6.32, up 5%. Tariffs cost about $0.16 per share (Item 7, Earnings per share).
- Operating cash flow: $3,027M. Capex: $819M. FCF is $2,207.6M, a 9.7% margin (XBRL screen). Operating cash flow minus capex from the MD&A gives $2,208M, which agrees. Cash flow was helped by accounts payable leverage rising to 91% from 87% (Item 7, Operating Activities). That is partly timing, so FCF may be flattered.
- FCF margin averages 7.9% (XBRL screen), so fiscal 2025 sits above its own average. Normalized FCF yield is 2.5% (XBRL screen).
- Net debt: negative, which means net cash. Cash was $4.6B against $1.5B of Senior Notes, with $500M due April 2026 (Item 7, Liquidity). XBRL net debt/FCF is -1.17 and net debt/EBITDA is -0.80, which implies roughly $2.6B net cash on the screen's definition. Operating leases of $3.9B are not included in that figure (Contractual Obligations).
- Share count: diluted shares fell about 2% in fiscal 2025. 7.1M shares were repurchased for $1,050M at an average $147.61. The XBRL dilution figure is -6.0%, meaning shrinkage (XBRL screen). The average buyback price of $147.61 is well below the current $223.26. Net income divided by EPS implies roughly 324M diluted shares (derived).
- Dividends: $528.1M paid in fiscal 2025. The quarterly dividend is $0.4450, up from $0.4050 (Item 7, Dividends).
- Outlook (10-K): operating margin "slightly higher" in fiscal 2026. Capex rises to about $1.1B from $819M (Item 7). That will cut FCF unless operating cash flow keeps growing.
- Growth rates: revenue CAGR 6.7%, FCF CAGR 28.6% (XBRL screen). The FCF CAGR starts from a depressed base and should not be extrapolated.

## Why is it cheap?
It is not cheap. The composite screen is 0.67, but the valuation sub-score is only 0.32 (XBRL screen).
- EV/EBIT is 25.4x, which is 1.29 times its own history (XBRL screen). FCF yield is 3.1% on market cap of $71.3B (XBRL screen).
- The stock is near its highs: last close $223.26, up 29% over one year and 78% over three, 12% below its 5-year high (price stats).
- The worries the market does price in are tariffs (over half of goods come from China), a low-income consumer, and margin erosion from distribution-center costs. The bull case is that the guidance raise and the off-price share-gain story justify the premium.
- My view: the worries are real but modest. Tariffs cost $0.16 per share in fiscal 2025, and the company expects better margin in 2026. But the stock is priced as if everything goes right. The screen flags quality, not cheapness.

cyclical_normalized = False, so no normalized-peak adjustment is applied. Still, retail is sensitive to the consumer. The screen's normalized figures (FCF yield 2.5%, EV/EBIT 26.2x) are slightly worse than the reported ones, which shows that fiscal 2025 FCF was somewhat above average.

## Disruption risk
Not mandatory (not a software, payments or internet name). The 10-K cites e-commerce and AI-enabled competitors as a risk, but Ross is stores-only and the off-price treasure-hunt model is relatively insulated (Item 1A, Competition).

## Risks (top 5, most important first)
1. Tariffs and trade policy. More than half of the goods sold originate from China. Higher tariffs raise costs and could force changes in sourcing. The impact on fiscal 2025 merchandise margin was about 20 bps (10-K, Item 1A; Item 7).
2. Consumer spending and macro. Inflation, unemployment, credit and policy changes hit its value-seeking customer, and sales volatility is hard to offset with fixed costs (Item 1A).
3. Merchandise supply. The model depends on buyers getting branded goods at deep discounts. Better inventory control by vendors, or vendors refusing to sell, would reduce supply (Item 1A, Strategic Risks).
4. Competition with low entry barriers. Other off-price chains, department stores, mass merchants and online players all compete, and AI adoption could favor competitors that execute faster (Item 1A).
5. Expansion execution. New markets, such as New York and Puerto Rico, may take longer to reach target profits. The company is also building distribution centers (Buckeye opened May 2025, Randleman under construction), which already added about 25 bps of distribution cost (Item 1A; Item 7). The remaining Item 1A risks (labor, weather and others) are only partly in the excerpt.

## Valuation
- FCF yield: 3.1% reported, 2.5% normalized (XBRL screen). With fiscal 2026 capex rising to about $1.1B, near-term FCF yield may fall unless earnings grow.
- EV/EBIT: 25.4x, versus 26.2x on normalized EBIT, and 1.29 times its own history (XBRL screen). The stock trades at a premium to its own past.
- What is priced in: roughly 7-8% annual growth in earnings for years, margins held near 12%, and continued buybacks, with no margin damage from tariffs. A 3% FCF yield plus 5-7% growth gives a low double-digit expected return at best, with a worst 5-year drawdown of 38% in the price history (price stats).
- Third-party "fair value" headlines (one claims 199% upside) are low quality and I do not use them.

## Thesis
- A consistent compounder: revenue CAGR 6.7%, ROIC 59%, positive comps for several years.
- Net cash and no solvency risk: about $4.6B of cash against $1.5B of notes (Item 7).
- Unit growth of about 5% a year (110 new stores in 2026) plus comp growth supports high-single-digit sales growth, and dd's growth is reaccelerating.
- Capital return: $1.05B of buybacks a year, a new $2.55B two-year authorization, and a dividend that is rising about 10% a year.
- Management guides to slightly higher operating margin in fiscal 2026 as tariff and distribution-center drags fade.

For a buy, I would want a lower price (a better entry, such as an EV/EBIT in the high teens or a 4%+ FCF yield) or evidence that earnings are growing faster than the screen implies.

## What breaks the thesis
- Operating margin below 11.0% for two consecutive quarters, versus 11.9% in fiscal 2025 and guidance for slightly more.
- Comparable store sales negative for two quarters, or traffic falling.
- Merchandise margin down more than 50 bps from tariffs or buying costs.
- Inventory growing faster than sales, or packaway above 45% of inventory with rising markdowns.
- Capex above $1.1B without a matching rise in operating cash flow, so FCF margin falls below 7% (its 5-year average is 7.9%).
