Census or survey?

Populationall 30 students in class 1/2

Population
The whole group we want to learn about.
Census
Collect data from every member of the population.
Sample · Survey
A sample is a part of the population. A survey collects data from a sample only.
Our question: "What snack do students in 1/2 like best?" The population is the whole group we want to know about: all 30 students in 1/2. Beat 1, census: we ask every one of the 30 students (all icons turn blue). Beat 2, survey: we ask only a sample, 6 of the 30 (the coral icons move down into the sample box). 6 out of 30 = 6/30 = 1/5 = 20% of the class. Small group, or must be exact → census. Big group, little time or money → survey.
Compare the bars. A census asks everyone, so it takes more time and costs more, but the result is exact. A survey asks only a sample: quicker and cheaper, and the result is close (an estimate) if the sample is chosen fairly. Then the two real examples. Census: Thailand's National Statistical Office runs a population and housing census about every 10 years and tries to count every person. Survey: the canteen asks 100 of 2,400 students; 100 ÷ 2,400 = 1/24, about 4% of the school. Asking all 2,400 would take far too long. Beat 1: the rule of thumb. A pictogram uses pictures to show data. Beat 1: the title tells us what it shows. Beat 2: the labels: one row for each day. Beat 3: the key: one cup stands for 10 cups. Lesson 3 · Bar charts

The parts of a bar chart

A bar chart uses bars to compare groups. A good one has:

  1. A title and axis labels
  2. A scale with equal steps
  3. Bars of the same width
  4. Equal gaps between bars
A bar chart compares groups with bars. The taller the bar, the more students. Beat 1: the title and the two axis labels (Way to school, Number of students) say what is shown. Beat 2: the scale goes up in equal steps of 2: 0, 2, 4 … 14. Beat 3: every bar has the same width, so only the height matters. Beat 4: the gaps between bars are equal too. Read the chart: School van 12, Car 9, BTS 7, Motorbike 5, Walk 3. Total 12 + 9 + 7 + 5 + 3 = 36 students (12 + 9 = 21, 21 + 7 = 28, 28 + 5 = 33, 33 + 3 = 36 ✓). Ask: "How many more come by school van than by car?" (12 − 9 = 3).

Check: 126 ÷ 360 = 0.35. The other angles: car 10 ÷ 40 × 360 = 90°, walk 8 ÷ 40 × 360 = 72°, BTS 6 ÷ 40 × 360 = 54°, bicycle 2 ÷ 40 × 360 = 18°. Sum: 126 + 90 + 72 + 54 + 18 = 360°.
