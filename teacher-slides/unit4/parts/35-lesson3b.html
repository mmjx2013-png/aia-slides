Pie chart: a circle cut into slices. Each slice is a part of the whole.
The whole circle is all of the data: 100%. One full turn around the centre is 360°. Click 1: cut it in half: 50% = 180° (360 ÷ 2 = 180), a straight line. Click 2: cut a half again: 25% = 90° (360 ÷ 4 = 90), a right angle. Click 3: one tenth: 10% = 36° (360 ÷ 10 = 36). The pie is now 25% + 25% + 40% + 10% = 100%, and 90° + 90° + 144° + 36° = 360°. (40% of 360° = 144°.) Ask: "What angle is 1%?" 360 ÷ 100 = 3.6°. Key idea: the slices always add up to 100% and to 360°.

20 ÷ 100 × 400 = 80
2How many more like bubble tea than water?
160 − 60 = 100
400 students in M1 chose a favourite drink: bubble tea 40%, Thai tea 25%, pink milk 20%, water 15% (40 + 25 + 20 + 15 = 100%). To change a percent to a number of students: percent ÷ 100 × 400 (so 1% of 400 is 4 students, and 10% is 40). Let students try, then click. Click 1, Q1: 20 ÷ 100 × 400 = 80 students like pink milk. Click 2, Q2: bubble tea 40 ÷ 100 × 400 = 160; water 15 ÷ 100 × 400 = 60; 160 − 60 = 100 more. Quicker way: 40% − 15% = 25%, and 25% of 400 = 100. Check all: 160 + 100 + 80 + 60 = 400. Thai tea = 25 ÷ 100 × 400 = 100.

Worked example. Data: how class 1/2 gets to school: school bus 14, car 12, walk 8, BTS 6. Rule: angle = value ÷ total × 360°. Click 1: total = 14 + 12 + 8 + 6 = 40 students. Click 2: bus = 14 ÷ 40 × 360° = 126° (360 ÷ 40 = 9° for each student, and 14 × 9 = 126). Click 3: car 12 × 9 = 108°, walk 8 × 9 = 72°, BTS 6 × 9 = 54°. Check: 126 + 108 + 72 + 54 = 360°. Click 4: draw it. Start with a line from the centre to 12 o'clock, put the protractor on that line, mark 126°, and go on around the circle, slice by slice. Ask: "What should the angles always add up to?" (360°).
