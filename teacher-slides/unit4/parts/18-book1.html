Book p.167, items 10, 12 and 17. Students tap Yes or No on each card and explain first. Then click through the method. Beat 1 · LOOK: what is each question about? Item 10: ONE oak tree. Item 12: ONE office, at one moment. Item 17: ONE painting. Beat 2 · THINK: check it once and you are done. The drawing shows 6 branches (just an example), 3 people wearing ties, and the Mona Lisa is about 500 years old (painted about 1503–1519, so about 507–523 years by 2026). Each question has ONE number as its answer. Beat 3 · WHY: anyone who checks gets the same answer. Nothing varies, so there is no set of data to collect. Watch out: starting with “How many…?” or “How old…?” does not make a question statistical. Beat 4 · ANSWER: 10, 12, 17 are NOT statistical. Item 12 is open to debate. Book-style answer: not statistical, because the question asks for one count in one office (one number). Some people say it is statistical, because you must check each person (tie or no tie) and those answers vary. Accept either answer if the student explains it well. To make it clearly statistical: “How many people in the office wear a tie on each day of this week?” (Our answer key is not in the photos; this follows the same rule the book uses for item 13: one total = one answer.)

Find the time words: each day in 11, this month in 13.
Every day has its own number. 30 days give 30 numbers, and they vary: 2, 5, 9, 11 …
Add all the days together: one total, 166 cars. One number.
Both are about cars. Ask: at the end, one answer or many?
Answer
11 statistical · 13 not
Book p.167, items 11 and 13. The two questions look almost the same. Students tap Yes or No for each and explain. Beat 1 · LOOK: the time words. Item 11 says “each day this month”. Item 13 says “this month” only. Beat 2 · THINK (item 11): every day has its own number of cars sold, so we get a list of 30 numbers (one month of 30 days). In our example they go from 2 to 11 cars a day. The answers vary, so we need data: item 11 IS statistical. Beat 3 · THINK (item 13): add all the days together and you get ONE total. Example total: 4 + 6 + 3 + 5 + 2 + 7 + 9 = 36 (days 1–7), 5 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 10 = 41 (days 8–14), 4 + 5 + 2 + 6 + 4 + 7 + 9 = 37 (days 15–21), 3 + 5 + 4 + 6 + 5 + 8 + 11 = 42 (days 22–28), 6 + 4 = 10 (days 29–30). 36 + 41 + 37 + 42 + 10 = 166 cars ✓. One question, one number: item 13 is NOT statistical. Beat 4 · WATCH OUT: both questions need counting. The test is how many answers you get at the end. Answer: 11 statistical, 13 not statistical. The chart numbers are our own example; the book gives no data for these items.
