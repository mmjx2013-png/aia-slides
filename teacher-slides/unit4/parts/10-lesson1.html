To answer B, we must collect data from the whole class.
Beat 0: read both questions. Ask: "How many answers does each question have?" Question A is about one person ("I"), so it has one answer: 6:15. Beat 1: six friends answer question B: 5:45, 6:00, 6:15, 6:30, 6:45, 7:15. All different! Then everyone in the class answers and the dots drop: each dot is one student (coral = our 6 friends, blue = the other 11). Count per time: 5:30 1, 5:45 2, 6:00 3, 6:15 4, 6:30 3, 6:45 2, 7:00 1, 7:15 1, 7:30 0. Check: 1+2+3+4+3+2+1+1+0 = 17 students (6 coral + 11 blue = 17). Ask: "What is the most common wake-up time?" 6:15 (4 students). Beat 2: A has one answer. B has many answers that vary, so we must collect data to answer it.

Definition
A statistical question expects answers that vary. We answer it by collecting data from a group.
Beat 0: one question, asked to the 12 members of the art club. Beat 1: 12 members give 12 answers, and they are not all the same. The answers vary. Beat 2: we collect the answers as data and count them: Bus 4, BTS 3, Car 2, Walk 2, Bike 1. Check: 4 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 12 members. (Pills: Bus at 1st, 4th, 8th, 12th = 4; BTS 3rd, 7th, 11th = 3; Car 5th, 10th = 2; Walk 2nd, 9th = 2; Bike 6th = 1.) Beat 3: read the definition together. New term: statistical question. Ask: "Which two words matter most?" vary and data.

Use the test question at the top: "How long do students in 1/2 take to get to school?" Beat 1 · About a group: yes, it asks about all the students in 1/2, not one person. Beat 2 · Answers vary: yes, one student lives close (5 min), another far away (45 min). Beat 3 · Needs data: yes, we must ask every student and record the times. Four topics, two questions each. Before each beat, name the topic and ask students to make up both questions themselves. Beat 1 · Canteen: one bowl of noodles has one price (for example 40 baht), so one answer. Spending per student varies (20 baht, 45 baht, 60 baht …). Beat 2 · Sports day: Krit has one time. All M1 runners have many different times. Beat 3 · Rain: "Did it rain today?" is yes or no, one answer. Rain each day in September gives 30 amounts (September has 30 days), so the group is days, not people. Beat 4 · LINE stickers: Ploy is one person. The whole class gives many answers. The small pictures: one dot = one answer; many dots = many answers that vary.

B: the canteen opens at one time.
Students tap a choice (or come to the board and point) and explain why. Then click. Q1 (beat 1): C. "How long do 1/2 students spend on homework?" is about a group and the times vary. A (one gate), B (always 8 legs) and D (one bus colour) each have one answer. Q2 (beat 2): B. The canteen opens at one fixed time (for example 7:00), so there is only one answer and no data to collect. D is a trap: questions about time CAN be statistical, for example "What time do students in 1/2 wake up?"
